Qulipta cost, coupon & copay card

Qulipta (atogepant) is a once-daily oral gepant taken to prevent migraines.

The short answer

Qulipta costs about $1,100 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 with the Qulipta copay card. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 Part D cap.

Qulipta is a preventive, taken daily rather than at attack onset. As an oral CGRP option it competes with the injectable antibodies (Ajovy, Aimovig) — the choice often comes down to coverage and whether you prefer a daily pill or a monthly injection.

Qulipta cost with Medicare

Qulipta is not on the Medicare negotiated-price list. Part D members pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 annual cap. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Cash-pay strategies for Qulipta

Without insurance, compare discount cards and apply to myAbbVie Assist if income-eligible. Because Qulipta competes with cheaper generic preventives (like topiramate or propranolol), ask your prescriber whether a generic preventive is worth trying first if cost is a concern.

Cheaper alternatives to Qulipta

Ajovy and Aimovig are monthly injectable CGRP preventives — same goal, different route. Generic oral preventives (topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline) are far cheaper and are usually required first by insurers.

Preventive migraine care is where prior authorization bites hardest. Insurers almost always want to see that a cheap generic preventive was tried before they'll cover Qulipta. If your prescription is stuck, that documented step-therapy history is nearly always the missing piece — not the price.

Qulipta cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Qulipta without insurance?

About $1,100 a month at list price. Income-eligible uninsured patients can get it free through myAbbVie Assist.

How do I get Qulipta for $0?

With commercial insurance, the Qulipta copay card can bring you to as little as $0 a month. Not usable with Medicare or Medicaid.

Does Medicare cover Qulipta?

Generally under Part D with prior authorization. You pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 cap; the copay card is not usable on Medicare.

Qulipta or Ajovy — which is cheaper?

Both copay cards can reach $0 for commercial patients, so cost is usually a wash; the real difference is a daily pill (Qulipta) versus a monthly injection (Ajovy). Coverage on your plan often decides it.

Do I have to try other drugs first?

Usually yes. Insurers typically require step therapy — documented trials of cheaper generic preventives — before covering Qulipta.
