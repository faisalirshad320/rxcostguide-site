Nurtec vs Ubrelvy: cost, coverage & which is right for you

Two oral gepants for migraine, compared on cost, coverage and the key clinical difference — one also prevents migraines, the other only treats attacks.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

List prices are similar — both around $1,000 a month. Both reach $0 for commercially insured patients with their copay cards, and neither has a generic. The real difference is use: Nurtec treats attacks and can prevent them (every-other-day dosing), while Ubrelvy treats attacks only. Coverage and that dual use usually decide it, not price.

When Nurtec tends to be the choice

Nurtec’s advantage is versatility: the same drug can stop an attack and, taken every other day, help prevent migraines — useful for people who want one medicine for both. It dissolves on the tongue without water, which some patients prefer during an attack with nausea.

When Ubrelvy tends to be the choice

Ubrelvy is purely an acute treatment, and some patients and insurers prefer it for that focused role; a second dose can be taken in a day if needed. For someone who only needs to treat occasional attacks, it is a clean fit, and the copay card makes it $0 for commercial patients.

Which is cheaper for you?

Commercial insurance: both reach $0 with their cards — your plan’s preferred gepant decides. Medicare: neither is negotiated; both are covered subject to formulary under the $2,100 cap, and cards cannot be used. Uninsured: similar list prices; check each manufacturer’s assistance program. If you need both acute and preventive coverage from one drug, Nurtec’s dual role may be the deciding factor.

FAQ

Is Nurtec or Ubrelvy cheaper?

List prices are similar (~$1,000/month) and both reach $0 for commercially insured patients with their copay cards. The cheaper one is whichever your plan prefers.

What is the main difference between Nurtec and Ubrelvy?

Nurtec can both treat migraine attacks and prevent them (every-other-day dosing); Ubrelvy only treats attacks. Both are oral gepants.

Can I take both Nurtec and Ubrelvy?

They are the same class, so they are not usually combined. Which one — or whether to pair an acute gepant with a separate preventive — is a decision for your prescriber.
