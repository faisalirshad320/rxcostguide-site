Emgality cost, coupon & savings card

Emgality (galcanezumab) is a once-monthly self-injected CGRP antibody for migraine prevention and for episodic cluster headache.

The short answer

Emgality costs about $700 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can often get the first month free and then pay $0 with the Emgality savings card. There is no biosimilar. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap; the uninsured can apply to Lilly Cares.

Emgality is a preventive migraine treatment, taken once a month to reduce how often migraines happen — different from the acute gepants (Nurtec, Ubrelvy) you take when an attack starts. Insurers usually require you to have tried cheaper preventives first.

Emgality cost with Medicare

Emgality has no Medicare-negotiated price. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their Part D plan cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket maximum. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare. Plans typically require step therapy (trying cheaper preventives first).

Patient assistance for Emgality

Lilly provides Emgality free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through Lilly Cares. Proof of income and a prescription are required.

Lilly Cares Foundation

Cash-pay strategies for Emgality

There is no generic or biosimilar galcanezumab, so cash payers should use the savings card if commercially insured, apply to Lilly Cares if uninsured, or ask about cheaper oral preventives (topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline are inexpensive generics). Other CGRP antibodies (Aimovig, Ajovy) have similar prices and their own cards.

Cheaper alternatives to Emgality

Other CGRP antibodies — Aimovig and Ajovy — are comparable preventives with their own savings cards. Oral gepants like Qulipta also prevent migraine. Cheap generic preventives (topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline) are usually tried first. Clinical choice is your neurologist’s.

With the CGRP antibodies the prices are close enough that the deciding factors are usually your insurer’s preferred brand and the step-therapy hoops. I tell patients to let the prescriber’s office drive the prior authorization and to lean on the manufacturer card, because out of pocket these can be $0 for commercial patients — the paperwork, not the price, is the real obstacle.

Emgality cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Emgality with the savings card?

Often the first month is free and then $0 for commercially insured patients. The card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic for Emgality?

No — galcanezumab is a biologic with no biosimilar yet. Cheaper generic oral preventives (topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline) are alternatives your doctor may try first.

What does Emgality cost without insurance?

About $700 a month at list price. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can get it free through Lilly Cares.

Is Emgality the same as Nurtec?

No. Emgality is a monthly injection to prevent migraines; Nurtec is an oral gepant used to treat attacks (and, at a different schedule, to prevent them). They work differently.

Can I use the Emgality card on Medicare?

No — commercial insurance only. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.
