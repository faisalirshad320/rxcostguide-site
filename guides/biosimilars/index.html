Generics and biosimilars: the cheapest route when they exist

When a generic or biosimilar exists, it is almost always the cheapest way to get your medication. Here is how to tell and what to ask.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

A generic is an identical copy of a small-molecule drug; a biosimilar is a highly similar version of a biologic. Both must meet strict FDA standards and both are much cheaper than the brand. Humira now has multiple biosimilars, and generic apixaban, dabigatran, sacubitril/valsartan and others are reaching pharmacies.

Generic vs biosimilar — the difference

Generics copy simple chemical drugs (like statins or apixaban) exactly and are usually the cheapest option by far. Biosimilars copy complex biologic drugs (like Humira or Enbrel) so closely that the FDA finds no clinically meaningful difference — they can't be atom-identical because biologics are grown, not synthesized, but they work the same.

How much they save

Generics often cost a small fraction of the brand — sometimes 80–90% less. Biosimilars typically cut a biologic's price by 15–50% and, crucially, create competition that pushes all versions down over time.

The Humira example

Humira (adalimumab) now has numerous biosimilars on the US market at substantially lower prices. Some are "interchangeable," meaning a pharmacist may substitute them like a generic. If you are on Humira, ask your prescriber and pharmacist which biosimilar your plan prefers — it can cut your cost sharply.

What to ask your pharmacist

Two plain questions save the most money: "Is there a generic or biosimilar for this?" and "Is it cheaper for me than the brand, with my coverage?" Sometimes the brand with a copay card is still cheaper for a commercially insured patient — so compare both.

FAQ

Are biosimilars as safe and effective as the brand?

Yes. The FDA approves a biosimilar only when it has no clinically meaningful differences from the reference biologic in safety, purity, and potency. They are used interchangeably in practice under a prescriber's guidance.

Is there a biosimilar for Humira?

Yes — multiple adalimumab biosimilars are available in the US, several at much lower prices, and some are interchangeable at the pharmacy. Ask which one your plan prefers.

Why is the generic sometimes not cheaper for me?

If you have commercial insurance and a strong copay card on the brand, the brand can occasionally cost you less than the generic. Always compare the two for your specific coverage.
