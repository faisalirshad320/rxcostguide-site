Patient assistance programs — free drugs for those who qualify

If you are uninsured and on a limited income, a patient-assistance program can often provide branded drugs at no cost. Here is how they work.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Patient-assistance programs (PAPs) are run by drug manufacturers to give free or deeply discounted medication to uninsured, income-eligible patients. They are different from copay cards: PAPs are for people without commercial insurance, and eligibility is based on household income.

What a PAP is

A manufacturer patient-assistance program supplies its own drug free (or nearly free) to patients who cannot afford it and have no adequate insurance. Nearly every major manufacturer runs one — BMS Access Support, Amgen Safety Net, the Novartis and J&J foundations, Lilly Cares, and more.

How to qualify

Eligibility usually requires: being a US resident, being uninsured or underinsured, and having a household income under a set multiple of the federal poverty level (often 300–500%). You will need proof of income (tax return or pay stubs) and a prescription from your doctor.

How to apply

Find the program on the manufacturer's site (linked on each of our drug pages), complete the application — often your doctor's office signs part of it — and submit proof of income. Decisions typically take one to three weeks. Approvals usually last a year and can be renewed.

Independent charity foundations

If you have insurance but still can't afford your share, independent charities (the PAN Foundation, HealthWell Foundation, Patient Advocate Foundation) offer disease-specific grants. Funds open and close, so apply early when a fund for your condition is open.

FAQ

Are patient-assistance programs really free?

For those who qualify, yes — the manufacturer provides the drug at no cost. You pay nothing for the medication itself, though your doctor's visits are separate.

Can I use a PAP if I have Medicare?

Sometimes. Manufacturer PAPs for the uninsured usually exclude Medicare patients, but independent charity foundations (PAN, HealthWell) can help with Medicare out-of-pocket costs when a relevant fund is open.

How long does approval take?

Usually one to three weeks. Some programs offer a bridge supply while your application is processed — ask when you apply.
