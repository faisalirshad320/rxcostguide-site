Prior authorization: how to get your prescription approved

Prior authorization is the paperwork step that most often stalls an expensive prescription. Knowing how it works gets you through it faster.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Prior authorization (PA) is when your insurer requires your doctor to justify a prescription before it will cover it. The most common cause of denial is missing documentation — usually proof that a cheaper drug was tried first. Getting the paperwork right the first time is what speeds approval, not appealing after the fact.

Why insurers require it

Insurers use PA to steer patients to lower-cost options first and to confirm an expensive drug is medically appropriate. It is most common for branded drugs with cheaper alternatives, biologics, and high-cost injectables.

The most common reasons PA is denied

In practice, most denials come down to: no record that a required cheaper drug ("step therapy") was tried; missing lab values or diagnosis codes; or a form filled in incompletely. The drug itself is usually not the problem — the documentation is.

How to get approved faster

Ask your doctor's office to submit the PA with the specific clinical evidence the plan wants (prior drugs tried, relevant lab results, diagnosis codes). Ask your pharmacy to flag when a PA is needed so it is started early. If denied, request the specific reason and file a peer-to-peer review or appeal addressing exactly that reason.

If you're denied

You have the right to appeal. Get the denial reason in writing, have your prescriber submit a letter of medical necessity addressing it, and escalate to an external review if the internal appeal fails. Many denials are overturned on appeal when the missing evidence is supplied.

FAQ

How long does prior authorization take?

Anywhere from a few hours to two weeks. Standard requests often take a few business days; urgent requests can be expedited. Starting early — before you run out of medication — matters.

Can the manufacturer help with prior authorization?

Yes. Many manufacturer support programs (e.g. Amgen SupportPlus) will help your doctor's office with benefits investigation and the PA paperwork. Look for a "support" or "access" program on the drug's official site.

What is step therapy?

Step therapy is when your plan requires you to try one or more cheaper drugs before it covers a more expensive one. Documented proof you tried (and how you responded to) those drugs is often what a PA needs.
