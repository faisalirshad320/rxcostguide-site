How to afford prescriptions with no insurance

Being uninsured doesn't mean paying list price. Here are the routes that actually work, cheapest first.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

With no insurance, your best routes are, in order: a generic or biosimilar if one exists; patient-assistance programs (free drugs for income-eligible patients); discount cards (GoodRx, SingleCare); Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs for many generics; and always asking for a 90-day supply. Manufacturer copay cards usually do not work for the uninsured.

1. Ask for the generic first

If a generic or biosimilar exists, it is almost always the cheapest option — sometimes a few dollars a month. Ask your prescriber to write for the generic and your pharmacist whether it's in stock.

2. Apply for patient assistance

If your drug is branded with no generic, and your income qualifies, a manufacturer patient-assistance program can provide it free. This is the single biggest lever for expensive branded drugs. See our patient-assistance guide.

3. Compare discount cards

GoodRx and SingleCare negotiate cash prices that often beat the pharmacy's own. Prices vary by pharmacy, so check a few. You can't combine them with insurance, but for the uninsured they're a reliable win.

4. Try Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs

Cost Plus Drugs sells many generics at cost plus a small markup, often far below retail. Not every drug is available, but for common generics it's frequently the cheapest source. Check whether your drug is listed.

5. Always ask for 90-day supplies

Ninety-day fills cut per-fill dispensing fees and mean fewer trips. On discount cards and cash prices alike, they usually lower your monthly average.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to get medication without insurance?

If a generic exists, that plus a discount card or Cost Plus Drugs is usually cheapest. For branded drugs with no generic, a manufacturer patient-assistance program can provide it free if your income qualifies.

Can I use a manufacturer copay card if I'm uninsured?

Usually no — copay cards require commercial insurance. Uninsured patients should look at patient-assistance programs and discount cards instead.

Is GoodRx better than insurance?

Sometimes. For cheap generics, a GoodRx price can beat an insurance copay. You must choose one per fill (you can't stack them). For the uninsured, discount cards are simply one of the main tools.
