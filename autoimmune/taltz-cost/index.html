Taltz cost, coupon & copay card

Taltz (ixekizumab) is an IL-17A inhibitor biologic for psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis.

The short answer

Taltz costs several thousand dollars a month at list price (roughly $7,000/month-equivalent depending on dosing). Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 with the Taltz Together copay card. Medicare patients are protected by the $2,100 Part D cap.

Taltz uses a loading schedule then maintenance dosing, so the per-month cost varies early on. The list price is high, but the copay card (commercial), the Part D cap (Medicare) and patient assistance (uninsured) keep actual costs down.

Taltz cost with Medicare

Taltz is not on the Medicare negotiated-price list. Part D members pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 annual cap. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Taltz

Lilly provides Taltz free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through the Lilly Cares Foundation.

Lilly Cares Foundation

Cash-pay strategies for Taltz

Uninsured, income-eligible patients should apply to Lilly Cares rather than pay list price. As with Cosentyx, the loading phase doesn't change what a commercial patient pays under the copay card.

Cheaper alternatives to Taltz

Cosentyx (secukinumab) is the other main IL-17 inhibitor; Skyrizi and Stelara (IL-23) and TNF inhibitors are alternatives depending on your condition. The choice is your specialist's.

Taltz and Cosentyx are close cousins clinically and on cost. When a patient asks which to choose, I steer the money question to the plan: whichever your insurer places on the better tier, or covers without a fight, is usually the one to start with — the copay cards make both roughly $0 for commercial patients anyway.

Taltz cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Taltz without insurance?

Several thousand dollars a month at list price. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can get it free through the Lilly Cares Foundation.

How do I get Taltz for $0?

With commercial insurance, the Taltz Together copay card can bring you to as little as $0. Not usable with Medicare or Medicaid.

Does Medicare cover Taltz?

Generally under Part D with prior authorization. The $2,100 annual cap limits your out-of-pocket cost; copay cards are not usable on Medicare.

Is there a biosimilar for Taltz?

Not yet in the US. The copay card, Part D cap, or Lilly Cares are the current routes to lower cost.

Why does the first month of Taltz cost more?

Taltz has a loading schedule with extra injections at the start. Under the copay card your share is still capped, so a commercial patient shouldn't pay more during loading — confirm with Taltz Together if a large bill appears.
