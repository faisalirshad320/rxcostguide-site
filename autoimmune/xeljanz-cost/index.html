Xeljanz cost, coupon & copay card

Xeljanz (tofacitinib) is an oral JAK inhibitor from Pfizer used to treat adults with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and ankylosing spondylitis.

Manufacturer copay card

The short answer

Xeljanz has a list price of about ~$4,500–6,300/mo. With commercial insurance, the Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card can bring your cost down to as little as $0 per month (annual maximum applies). On Medicare Part D, copay cards are not allowed, but your out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. A generic tofacitinib is also available and may be a lower-cost option.

Xeljanz is a pill taken by mouth, which sets it apart from the many injectable or infused biologics used for the same autoimmune conditions. Your actual cost depends mostly on your insurance type, your plan's formulary tier and prior-authorization rules, and whether you qualify for the manufacturer's copay card. Because a generic tofacitinib is available, some plans may prefer or require it over the brand.

The Xeljanz copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per month with the Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card.

Pfizer's Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card helps cover out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients with commercial (private or employer) insurance. The card is not valid for people with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. An annual maximum applies, so check the program terms to see how much support you can receive over the year.

Xeljanz cost with Medicare

Xeljanz is not among the drugs with a Medicare negotiated price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan, but in 2026 your total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100. Federal rules bar Medicare beneficiaries from using manufacturer copay cards, so the Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card cannot be applied. Ask your plan whether generic tofacitinib is covered at a lower tier.

Cash-pay strategies for Xeljanz

Without insurance, Xeljanz is expensive, with a list price of ~$4,500–6,300/mo. A generic tofacitinib is available and is typically worth comparing, and pharmacy discount cards may lower the cash price depending on the pharmacy. No manufacturer free-drug patient-assistance program is listed, so ask your prescriber and pharmacist about the most affordable option.

Cheaper alternatives to Xeljanz

Generic tofacitinib contains the same active ingredient as Xeljanz and may cost less. Other JAK inhibitors and biologic therapies are also used for these conditions; your clinician can help weigh effectiveness, safety, and coverage.

I tell patients to check two things before their first fill: whether their plan covers brand Xeljanz or prefers generic tofacitinib, and whether a prior authorization is needed. If you have commercial insurance, enroll in the copay card before you pick up the prescription so the savings apply right away. If you're on Medicare, remember the card won't work, but the 2026 out-of-pocket cap limits how much you'll pay over the year.

Xeljanz cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Xeljanz cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per month with the Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card. An annual maximum applies.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Xeljanz?

Yes, a generic tofacitinib is available. There is no biosimilar, because Xeljanz is a small-molecule pill rather than a biologic. Ask your pharmacist or plan whether the generic is covered and how its price compares.

How much is Xeljanz without insurance?

The list price of Xeljanz is about ~$4,500–6,300/mo. Generic tofacitinib and pharmacy discount cards may lower your cash cost. No manufacturer free-drug patient-assistance program is listed.

Does Medicare cover Xeljanz, and can I use the copay card?

Many Medicare Part D plans cover Xeljanz, but cost-sharing varies by plan. Your out-of-pocket Part D drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. The copay card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

How do I enroll in the Xeljanz Co-Pay Savings Card?

You can enroll through Pfizer's official patient affordability page at https://xeljanz.pfizerpro.com/r/coverage-support/patient-affordability. You must have commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible.
