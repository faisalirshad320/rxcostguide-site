Humira vs Skyrizi: cost, coverage & which is right for you

A TNF inhibitor with cheap biosimilars versus a newer IL-23 inhibitor with none — compared on what actually drives your out-of-pocket cost.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

On raw price, Humira now wins on cost because adalimumab biosimilars are available for a few hundred dollars a month, while Skyrizi has no biosimilar and lists around $21,000 per dose. But Skyrizi is dosed only every 8–12 weeks and both reach near $0–$5 for commercially insured patients with their manufacturer cards. The deciding factor is usually the condition and your plan’s preferred product, not price alone.

When Humira (or a biosimilar) tends to be the choice

Adalimumab is older, very well understood, and — crucially — now cheap thanks to biosimilars. For many conditions it is a reasonable first biologic, and a biosimilar can cost a fraction of any brand-only competitor. If cost is the priority, this is usually the side to look at first.

When Skyrizi tends to be the choice

Skyrizi targets IL-23 specifically and performs very well for plaque psoriasis and some IBD, often with infrequent dosing (every 8–12 weeks) that patients value. It is costly and biosimilar-free, but with commercial insurance and the Complete card the out-of-pocket cost is small.

Which is cheaper for you?

Commercial insurance: both reach near $0–$5 with their cards, so your plan’s preferred product decides. Medicare: neither is negotiated; a Humira biosimilar often lands on a lower tier, which can make adalimumab cheaper. Uninsured: an adalimumab biosimilar is far cheaper than Skyrizi; both have myAbbVie Assist. Never switch biologics for cost alone without your prescriber.

FAQ

Is Humira or Skyrizi cheaper?

Humira — because adalimumab biosimilars are now available cheaply, while Skyrizi has no biosimilar. With commercial insurance both reach near $0–$5 via their cards, so coverage often decides.

Are Humira and Skyrizi interchangeable?

No. They are different classes (TNF vs IL-23 inhibitor) for overlapping but not identical uses. Switching is a clinical decision for your prescriber.

Which is dosed less often?

Skyrizi — maintenance is every 8–12 weeks, versus every 1–2 weeks for Humira. Many patients value the less frequent injections.
