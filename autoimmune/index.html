What autoimmune medications cost, and how to pay less

Biologics and targeted immune drugs are among the most expensive prescriptions in America — often several thousand dollars a month. Copay cards, biosimilars and patient assistance are what make them affordable.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-05
The short answer

Branded autoimmune biologics run roughly $2,000 to $7,600 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can usually pay as little as $0–$5 with the manufacturer copay card. Enbrel has a Medicare-negotiated price (~$2,355) effective 2026, and biosimilars now cut the cost of drugs like Humira sharply. Uninsured patients rely on generous manufacturer patient-assistance programs.

Biosimilars are reshaping this category

Where a biosimilar exists (notably for Humira and Enbrel), it can cut the cost of the biologic substantially and is often the route your plan will prefer. See our generics & biosimilars guide and always ask which version your plan places on the best tier.

Copay cards and prior authorization

Every branded biologic here has a copay card that brings commercially insured patients to near $0 — but coverage almost always requires prior authorization, usually with documented step therapy. Getting that paperwork right is what unlocks the drug.
