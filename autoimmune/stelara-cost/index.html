Stelara cost, biosimilars & copay card

Stelara (ustekinumab) is an IL-12/23 inhibitor biologic for psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease — and, since 2025, it faces cheaper ustekinumab biosimilars.

The short answer

Brand Stelara lists around $25,000 per dose, dosed every 8–12 weeks in maintenance. Since 2025 ustekinumab biosimilars (Wezlana, Selarsdi, Pyzchiva and others) are available at lower prices. Commercially insured patients can reach $5 per dose with the Janssen withMe card; the biggest lever is now the biosimilar.

Like Humira before it, Stelara has entered the biosimilar era. Several ustekinumab biosimilars launched in 2025, and plans are increasingly steering patients to them. The cost question for most people is which ustekinumab product their plan prefers.

Stelara cost with Medicare

Stelara itself has no Medicare-negotiated price. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their plan cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual Part D out-of-pocket maximum for self-injected forms. Many Part D plans now prefer a lower-cost ustekinumab biosimilar — ask which your plan covers best.

Patient assistance for Stelara

J&J provides Stelara free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through its patient-assistance foundation. Proof of income and a prescription are required.

Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation

Cash-pay strategies for Stelara

Do not pay brand cash price. Ask your prescriber and pharmacist about a ustekinumab biosimilar, which is cheaper, or apply to the J&J Patient Assistance Foundation if uninsured and income-eligible. Prior authorization is usually required.

Cheaper alternatives to Stelara

The cheapest route is usually a ustekinumab biosimilar. Other biologics — Skyrizi (IL-23), Humira and biosimilars (TNF), Rinvoq (oral JAK) — treat overlapping conditions differently. Which is right is a clinical decision.

Stelara is now walking the path Humira walked: biosimilars have arrived and plans are moving patients toward them. For a stable patient, a switch to a biosimilar is usually straightforward and saves a great deal, but it should be done with the prescriber’s knowledge — not least because the dosing and the prior-authorization trail need to follow the new product.

Stelara cost — frequently asked questions

Is there a biosimilar for Stelara?

Yes — ustekinumab biosimilars (Wezlana, Selarsdi, Pyzchiva and others) launched in 2025 and are cheaper than brand Stelara. Several are interchangeable.

How much is Stelara with insurance?

Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $5 per dose with the Janssen withMe card, though a biosimilar may be cheaper. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 cap.

What does Stelara cost without insurance?

Brand Stelara is about $25,000 per dose. A biosimilar is cheaper, and income-eligible uninsured patients can get brand Stelara free through the J&J Patient Assistance Foundation.

How often is Stelara dosed?

After an induction dose, maintenance is typically every 8–12 weeks depending on the condition, so despite the high per-dose price it is given only a few times a year.

Should I switch to a Stelara biosimilar?

Often it saves a lot and biosimilars are rigorously evaluated, but any switch should be made with your prescriber, who manages dosing and the insurer paperwork.
