Rinvoq vs Humira: cost, coverage & which is right for you

An oral daily JAK inhibitor versus an injected TNF biologic with cheap biosimilars — compared on cost and coverage.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Both are expensive at list (Rinvoq ~$6,900/month, brand Humira ~$7,000/month), but Humira now has cheap biosimilars and Rinvoq does not. Commercially insured patients reach $0–$5/month on either card. The real differences are form (Rinvoq is a daily pill, Humira an injection) and your plan’s preference — not sticker price.

When Rinvoq tends to be the choice

Rinvoq is an oral pill, which many patients prefer over injections, and it can work when a TNF inhibitor has not. As a JAK inhibitor it carries a boxed warning and is often used after other therapies. It is brand-only, so cost relies on the card or assistance.

When Humira (or a biosimilar) tends to be the choice

Humira is often tried first and now has cheap biosimilars, making adalimumab the lower-cost option for many plans. If you are comfortable injecting and a TNF inhibitor suits your condition, it is frequently the economical choice.

Which is cheaper for you?

Commercial insurance: both reach near $0–$5 with their cards. Medicare: neither is negotiated, but a Humira biosimilar often sits on a lower tier. Uninsured: an adalimumab biosimilar is cheaper than Rinvoq; both brands have myAbbVie Assist. The choice between a daily pill and an injection is clinical — discuss it with your prescriber.

FAQ

Is Rinvoq or Humira cheaper?

Brand list prices are similar, but Humira has cheap biosimilars and Rinvoq does not, so adalimumab is usually the lower-cost option. With commercial insurance both reach near $0–$5 via their cards.

Is Rinvoq a pill or an injection?

Rinvoq is a once-daily oral tablet; Humira is a self-injection given every 1–2 weeks. Many patients choose based on that difference.

Can Rinvoq replace Humira?

They are different classes and Rinvoq is often used when a TNF inhibitor like Humira has not worked. Whether to switch is a clinical decision for your prescriber.
