Vtama cost, coupon & copay card

Vtama (tapinarof) is a nonsteroidal topical cream that activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor and is used to treat plaque psoriasis and atopic dermatitis (eczema).

The short answer

Vtama has a list price of ~$1,556 per 60 g tube. People with commercial insurance may pay as little as $0 to $35 per prescription with the Vtama Savings Card (commercial), subject to program terms. On Medicare Part D, the savings card cannot be used, but out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026.

Vtama is applied directly to affected skin and offers a nonsteroidal option for people with plaque psoriasis or eczema. Because it is a brand-name cream with a high list price, what you actually pay depends mainly on your insurance coverage, your plan's formulary placement, and whether you qualify for the manufacturer's savings card. The amount of cream you need, which depends on how much skin is affected, also affects how often you refill.

The Vtama copay card

The Vtama Savings Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 to $35 per prescription if you have commercial insurance.

The Vtama Savings Card is the manufacturer's copay program for people with commercial (private or employer) insurance. It is not available to anyone with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Program terms apply, so review the current terms on the official enrollment page before you fill.

Vtama cost with Medicare

Vtama has not been selected for Medicare drug price negotiation, so there is no negotiated maximum fair price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan. In 2026, total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 per year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the Vtama Savings Card cannot be used.

Cash-pay strategies for Vtama

Without insurance, Vtama costs about ~$1,556 per 60 g tube at list price. Prescription discount cards may lower the pharmacy cash price, and it is worth comparing prices across pharmacies. Because no manufacturer free-drug program is listed, ask your prescriber whether a lower-cost topical treatment could be appropriate for you.

Cheaper alternatives to Vtama

Other topical options for plaque psoriasis and eczema include corticosteroids, vitamin D analogues, calcineurin inhibitors, and other nonsteroidal creams. Several of these are available as lower-cost generics. Your prescriber can help weigh cost against effectiveness and side effects.

I tell patients to check their formulary before the first fill, because brand-name topicals like Vtama often require prior authorization or step therapy. If you have commercial insurance, enroll in the savings card before you go to the pharmacy, not after. If you are on Medicare, plan around the annual out-of-pocket cap and ask your prescriber whether a covered alternative would cost less.

Vtama cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Vtama cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, the Vtama Savings Card can bring your cost to as little as $0 to $35 per prescription. Program terms apply.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Vtama?

Vtama is a small-molecule cream, not a biologic, so biosimilars do not apply. Ask your pharmacist whether a generic tapinarof is available.

How much is Vtama without insurance?

The list price is about ~$1,556 per 60 g tube. Discount cards and comparing pharmacy prices may lower what you pay. No manufacturer free-drug program is listed.

Does Medicare cover Vtama, and can I use the savings card?

Coverage varies by Part D plan. The savings card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket costs are capped at $2,100 per year.

How do I sign up for the Vtama Savings Card?

Enroll through the official page at https://vtama.com/plaque-psoriasis/download/savings-card/. You must have commercial insurance to be eligible.
