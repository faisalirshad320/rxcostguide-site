Nubeqa cost, coupon & copay card

Nubeqa (darolutamide) is an oral androgen receptor inhibitor made by Bayer and used to treat adults with prostate cancer.

The short answer

Nubeqa has a list price of about ~$14,758/mo. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Nubeqa $0 Co-pay Program, which covers up to $25,000/yr. On Medicare Part D, the card cannot be used, but your 2026 out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100, and Bayer's patient assistance foundation may help eligible patients.

Nubeqa is taken by mouth every day, often alongside hormone therapy, and is usually continued long-term, so monthly costs add up quickly over a year. As a brand-only specialty oncology drug with no generic available, your price depends mostly on your insurance type, your plan's specialty-tier cost sharing, and whether you qualify for manufacturer or foundation help.

The Nubeqa copay card

Commercially insured patients can lower their Nubeqa cost with Bayer's Nubeqa $0 Co-pay Program.

Eligible patients may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Nubeqa $0 Co-pay Program, with the program covering up to $25,000/yr. It is for commercial insurance only — not Medicare or Medicaid or other government insurance. Enroll online before your next fill and give the card details to your specialty pharmacy.

Nubeqa cost with Medicare

Nubeqa is not on the Medicare drug price negotiation list, so there is no negotiated maximum fair price. Most Medicare patients get it through Part D, where costs vary by plan, but out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 in 2026. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare patients; instead, ask about the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan to spread costs over the year, and check whether you qualify for the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation or Extra Help.

Patient assistance for Nubeqa

The Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation may provide Nubeqa at no cost to eligible patients who are uninsured or whose coverage leaves them unable to afford the drug. Eligibility depends on income, insurance status, and other criteria; applications typically require your prescriber's involvement.

Cash-pay strategies for Nubeqa

Without insurance, Nubeqa's list price is about ~$14,758/mo, and pharmacy discount cards usually make only a small dent in specialty cancer drugs like this. The most meaningful option for uninsured patients is usually the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation. There is no generic darolutamide, so ask your oncologist whether another covered treatment option is appropriate if cost is a barrier.

Cheaper alternatives to Nubeqa

Other oral androgen receptor inhibitors and hormone-based therapies are also used for prostate cancer; which one fits depends on your disease stage, other medications, and insurance coverage, so discuss options with your oncologist.

I tell patients not to let the list price stop them from starting Nubeqa until we have checked their coverage, because most people end up paying far less through the copay program, the Medicare cap, or Bayer's foundation. I also suggest calling the specialty pharmacy before each refill to confirm the card or assistance is still applied, since new plan years and changes in insurance can quietly reset benefits. If you switch from commercial insurance to Medicare, plan ahead, because the copay card will stop working.

Nubeqa cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Nubeqa with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Nubeqa $0 Co-pay Program, up to $25,000/yr in program benefits. It is for commercial insurance only — not Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Nubeqa?

No. Nubeqa (darolutamide) is available only as a brand-name drug from Bayer. It is a small-molecule pill, so biosimilars do not apply, and no generic is currently available in the US.

How much does Nubeqa cost without insurance?

Nubeqa's list price is about ~$14,758/mo. Uninsured patients should apply to the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation, which may provide the medication at no cost if they qualify.

Does Medicare cover Nubeqa, and can I use the copay card?

Medicare Part D plans generally cover Nubeqa, with costs varying by plan, and your 2026 out-of-pocket costs for covered Part D drugs are capped at $2,100. The Nubeqa copay card cannot be used with Medicare, and Nubeqa is not a Medicare-negotiated drug.

How do I enroll in the Nubeqa copay program?

Enroll online at https://www.nubeqacopayprogram.com if you have commercial insurance, then give the card information to your specialty pharmacy. For income-based help, visit the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation at https://www.patientassistance.bayer.us/.
