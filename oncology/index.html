What cancer medications cost, and how to pay less

Modern targeted cancer therapies — the oral kinase inhibitors, hormone-pathway drugs and CDK4/6 inhibitors — carry some of the highest list prices in medicine, often $10,000 to $25,000 a month.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-13
The short answer

Most branded oral cancer drugs run $13,000 to $26,000 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can usually pay $0 to $10 a month with the manufacturer copay program. Medicare patients rely on Part D and the $2,100 out-of-pocket cap; uninsured patients should apply to each maker’s foundation, which often provides the drug free.

Why cancer drugs cost so much

Targeted oral oncology drugs are patent-protected with no generics, so list prices are extreme. For insured patients the manufacturer copay card is usually the difference between $0 and thousands a month.

Copay cards vs. foundations

Commercially insured patients use the manufacturer copay card ($0–$10/month on most of these). Medicare and Medicaid patients cannot use copay cards, but the maker’s patient-assistance foundation often provides the drug free to those who qualify by income.
