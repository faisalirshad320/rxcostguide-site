Kisqali cost, coupon & copay card

Kisqali (ribociclib) is an oral CDK4/6 inhibitor from Novartis, taken with hormone therapy by adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer.

The short answer

Kisqali's list price is about ~$15,000/mo (dose-dependent) before insurance. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer (an annual savings limit applies). On Medicare Part D, your out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026, and the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation may help eligible patients who are uninsured or underinsured.

Kisqali is taken by mouth, typically in combination with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant, and is often continued for long periods, so monthly costs add up over time. The price depends on the prescribed dose, and what you actually pay is driven mainly by your insurance type, your plan's specialty-tier rules, and whether you qualify for manufacturer savings or assistance.

The Kisqali copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer.

The Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer is a Novartis savings program that covers part or all of your out-of-pocket cost for Kisqali when you have commercial (private or employer) insurance. An annual savings limit applies, so very high-deductible plans may still leave some cost later in the year. The offer is not valid for patients with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. You can enroll through the official Kisqali patient support site.

Kisqali cost with Medicare

Kisqali is not among the drugs with a Medicare negotiated price, so there is no maximum fair price for it. It is generally covered under Medicare Part D, where the 2026 out-of-pocket cap of $2,100 limits what you pay for covered drugs in a year; because Kisqali is a high-cost specialty drug, many patients reach that cap early. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer cannot be used, but the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan can spread out-of-pocket costs across the year, and the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation may help eligible patients.

Patient assistance for Kisqali

The Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation may provide Kisqali at reduced or no cost to eligible patients who are uninsured or whose insurance does not adequately cover the drug. Eligibility is based on income and insurance status, and an application with prescriber input is usually required. Check current criteria and apply on the foundation's website.

Cash-pay strategies for Kisqali

Paying cash for Kisqali is very expensive, with a list price of about ~$15,000/mo (dose-dependent), and pharmacy discount cards usually offer limited savings on brand-only specialty cancer drugs. If you are uninsured, the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation is typically the most important option to explore. Ask your oncology team or specialty pharmacy about financial counselors and independent cancer charities, and whether another CDK4/6 inhibitor might be more affordable under your circumstances.

Cheaper alternatives to Kisqali

Other oral CDK4/6 inhibitors are also used with hormone therapy for HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer; costs and coverage differ by plan, so ask your oncologist and insurer which option is preferred on your formulary.

I tell patients to get their benefits checked before the first fill, because Kisqali usually sits on a specialty tier and the cost difference between commercial coverage with the copay offer and Medicare can be large. Kisqali also requires regular monitoring, including ECGs for heart rhythm (QT interval) and blood tests for liver function, so I encourage patients to factor those visits into their planning. If cost becomes a barrier, I would rather they call the clinic early than skip doses—there are usually assistance options worth trying.

Kisqali cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Kisqali cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer. An annual savings limit applies, and the offer is for commercial insurance only — not Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Kisqali?

No. Ribociclib is a small-molecule pill, so a biosimilar does not apply, and Kisqali is currently sold as a brand-name product from Novartis. Ask your pharmacist about current generic availability.

How much is Kisqali without insurance?

Kisqali's list price is about ~$15,000/mo (dose-dependent). Uninsured patients should look into the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation (https://pap.novartis.com/), which may provide the drug at reduced or no cost to those who qualify.

Does Medicare cover Kisqali, and what will I pay?

Kisqali is generally covered under Medicare Part D, and in 2026 your out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 for the year. Kisqali does not have a Medicare negotiated price, and manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Kisqali copay program?

You can enroll in the Kisqali $0 Co-Pay Plus Offer through the official Kisqali patient support site at https://support.kisqali.com/. You must have commercial insurance; patients with Medicare or Medicaid are not eligible.
