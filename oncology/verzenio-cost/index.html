Verzenio cost, coupon & copay card

Verzenio (abemaciclib) is an oral CDK4/6 inhibitor from Eli Lilly used together with hormone (endocrine) therapy to treat adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer.

The short answer

Verzenio has a list price of about ~$18,000/mo, but most commercially insured patients pay far less: the Verzenio Savings Card can bring the cost down to as little as $0 per 28-day prescription, with a program maximum of up to $10,600/yr. On Medicare Part D, the savings card cannot be used, but out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 for 2026. Uninsured or underinsured patients can apply to the Lilly Cares Foundation.

Verzenio is taken by mouth as a continuous daily regimen, usually alongside an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant, and treatment often continues for months to years. Because it is a brand-only specialty oncology drug with a high list price, what you actually pay depends heavily on your insurance type, your plan's specialty-tier coinsurance, and whether you qualify for the manufacturer's savings card or patient assistance. It is typically dispensed through a specialty pharmacy, and prior authorization is commonly required.

The Verzenio copay card

The Verzenio Savings Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per 28-day prescription if you have commercial insurance.

The savings card is offered by Eli Lilly and is applied at the pharmacy after your commercial plan pays its share. Total savings are limited to up to $10,600/yr, so patients with very high deductibles or coinsurance may still owe something later in the year. The card is for commercial insurance only and cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance.

Verzenio cost with Medicare

Verzenio is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price, so there is no maximum fair price for it. It is generally covered under Medicare Part D, where your out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 for 2026; once you reach that cap, you pay nothing further for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards such as the Verzenio Savings Card for Medicare patients, but you can ask your plan about spreading costs into monthly payments through the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan, and lower-income beneficiaries may qualify for Extra Help.

Patient assistance for Verzenio

The Lilly Cares Foundation is Eli Lilly's patient assistance program for people who are uninsured or underinsured and meet its eligibility criteria. Eligible patients may receive Verzenio at reduced or no cost. Your oncology team or a financial navigator can help you complete the application, which typically requires prescriber information and proof of income.

Cash-pay strategies for Verzenio

Without insurance, Verzenio's list price is about ~$18,000/mo, which is out of reach for most patients. Pharmacy discount cards usually offer only modest savings on specialty cancer drugs, so the better route is to apply to the Lilly Cares Foundation and ask your cancer center's financial counselor about independent charitable foundations. There is no generic version of Verzenio available in the US.

Cheaper alternatives to Verzenio

Other oral CDK4/6 inhibitors are used for HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, but they are not interchangeable without your oncologist's input because indications, dosing, and side-effect profiles differ. If cost or coverage is a barrier, ask your oncologist whether another agent in this class is preferred on your plan's formulary.

I tell patients not to let the list price be the reason they skip or delay Verzenio — very few people actually pay it once insurance, the savings card, or Lilly Cares is in place. I also remind them that diarrhea is common early on, so having an antidiarrheal on hand and calling the team promptly helps avoid missed doses and wasted fills. If you are on Medicare, plan for the annual out-of-pocket cap and ask your plan about spreading payments across the year.

Verzenio cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Verzenio cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, the Verzenio Savings Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per 28-day prescription, up to a program maximum of up to $10,600/yr. It is not available to people on Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Verzenio?

No. There is no generic abemaciclib available in the US, and because Verzenio is a small-molecule tablet rather than a biologic, a biosimilar does not apply. It is available only as brand-name Verzenio.

How much is Verzenio without insurance?

The list price is about ~$18,000/mo. If you are uninsured or underinsured, apply to the Lilly Cares Foundation (https://www.lillycares.com/), which may provide the medicine at reduced or no cost to eligible patients.

Does Medicare cover Verzenio, and what will I pay?

Verzenio is generally covered under Medicare Part D, though costs vary by plan. Your out-of-pocket Part D spending is capped at $2,100 for 2026. Verzenio does not have a Medicare-negotiated price, and the Verzenio Savings Card cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Verzenio Savings Card?

Enroll on Lilly's official savings page at https://verzenio.lilly.com/savings-support. You must have commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible. Give the card information to your specialty pharmacy so it is applied to each fill.
