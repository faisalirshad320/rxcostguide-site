Linzess cost, coupon & copay card

Linzess (linaclotide) is a once-daily oral guanylate cyclase-C agonist for adults with irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C) or chronic idiopathic constipation.

The short answer

Linzess has a list price of about ~$550/mo. With commercial insurance, the Linzess Savings Program can bring your cost to $30 per 30- or 90-day supply (not $0), for up to 12 fills/yr. On Medicare Part D, the copay card cannot be used, but your total out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026, and myAbbVie Assist may help if you qualify.

Linzess is taken by mouth once a day, on an empty stomach, to increase intestinal fluid and ease constipation and abdominal discomfort. Because it is a brand-name drug that is usually taken long term, the list price adds up quickly, and what you actually pay depends heavily on your insurance type, your plan's formulary and deductible, and whether you use the manufacturer's savings program.

The Linzess copay card

Commercially insured patients can pay $30 per 30- or 90-day supply with the Linzess Savings Program (not $0).

The Linzess Savings Program lowers your out-of-pocket cost for each fill when you have commercial (private or employer) insurance. The program covers up to 12 fills/yr. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Enroll through the official Linzess savings page and show the card to your pharmacist when you fill your prescription.

Linzess cost with Medicare

Linzess is not among the drugs with a Medicare negotiated price, so your Part D cost depends on your plan's formulary tier, deductible, and coinsurance. In 2026, total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 per year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare patients, so the Linzess Savings Program cannot be used. Patients with limited income may want to check eligibility for myAbbVie Assist or Medicare Extra Help.

Patient assistance for Linzess

myAbbVie Assist is AbbVie's patient assistance program for eligible patients who are uninsured, underinsured, or unable to afford their medicine. Eligibility is based on criteria such as income and insurance status. Review the requirements on the program's eligibility page and ask your prescriber's office for help with the application.

Cash-pay strategies for Linzess

Without insurance, Linzess has a list price of about ~$550/mo, though pharmacy cash prices vary. Prescription discount cards may lower the price somewhat, so compare prices at several pharmacies. If cost is a barrier, apply to myAbbVie Assist or ask your clinician whether a lower-cost treatment could work for you.

Cheaper alternatives to Linzess

Other options for IBS-C and chronic idiopathic constipation include other prescription constipation medicines and lower-cost over-the-counter approaches such as fiber supplements and osmotic laxatives. Ask your clinician which option fits your symptoms and budget.

I tell patients to take Linzess at least 30 minutes before the first meal of the day, because taking it with food tends to worsen diarrhea, which is its most common side effect. If you are commercially insured, ask your prescriber for a 90-day supply, because the savings program charges the same $30 for a 30- or 90-day fill. If diarrhea is severe or you feel dizzy or dehydrated, stop the medicine and call your clinician rather than simply pushing through.

Linzess cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Linzess cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance and the Linzess Savings Program, you pay $30 per 30- or 90-day supply (not $0), for up to 12 fills/yr.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Linzess?

Biosimilars do not apply to Linzess. Availability of a generic linaclotide can change, so ask your pharmacist whether one is available and covered by your plan.

How much does Linzess cost without insurance?

The list price is about ~$550/mo. Actual cash prices vary by pharmacy, and discount cards or the myAbbVie Assist program may lower your cost if you qualify.

Can I use the Linzess copay card with Medicare?

No. The Linzess Savings Program is for commercial insurance only, not Medicare or Medicaid. On Medicare Part D, your cost depends on your plan, and total out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026.

How do I enroll in the Linzess Savings Program?

Enroll on the official Linzess savings page at https://www.linzess.com/savings-and-support#savings, then show the card to your pharmacist when you fill your prescription. You must have commercial insurance to qualify.
