Xifaxan cost, coupon & copay card

Xifaxan (rifaximin) is an oral antibiotic that stays largely within the gut and is prescribed for adults with IBS-D, hepatic encephalopathy, or traveler's diarrhea.

The short answer

Xifaxan's list price is about ~$2,000–4,000/mo (550 mg) without insurance. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Xifaxan Savings Card. On Medicare Part D, the card can't be used, but your annual out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026.

Rifaximin is poorly absorbed into the bloodstream, so it acts mainly inside the intestine. That makes it useful for IBS-D, traveler's diarrhea, and reducing the risk of recurrent hepatic encephalopathy. Cost depends heavily on the indication: IBS-D and traveler's diarrhea are usually treated with short courses, while hepatic encephalopathy typically requires ongoing daily therapy. Insurers often require prior authorization because the list price is high.

The Xifaxan copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Xifaxan Savings Card.

The Xifaxan Savings Card from Salix (Bausch Health) helps cover out-of-pocket costs for patients with commercial or private insurance. Enroll online, then show the card at the pharmacy with your prescription. The card is not valid for patients with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Program terms and any benefit limits can change, so review the current terms when you enroll.

Xifaxan cost with Medicare

Xifaxan is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan, and prior authorization is common. In 2026, Part D caps annual out-of-pocket prescription drug costs at $2,100, which limits what you pay once you reach the cap. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the Xifaxan Savings Card cannot be used; the Bausch Health Patient Assistance Program may be an option if you qualify.

Patient assistance for Xifaxan

Bausch Health's patient assistance program may provide Xifaxan at reduced or no cost to eligible patients who are uninsured or underinsured, based on income and other criteria. You'll typically need to apply with your prescriber's help and provide documentation of income and insurance status.

Cash-pay strategies for Xifaxan

Without insurance, Xifaxan's list price is about ~$2,000–4,000/mo (550 mg), and pharmacy discount cards may lower the cash price somewhat. If you can't afford it, apply to the Bausch Health Patient Assistance Program. A generic rifaximin is FDA-approved but not widely available, so ask your pharmacist whether it can be ordered.

Cheaper alternatives to Xifaxan

Depending on the condition, your clinician may consider other treatments, such as alternative IBS-D therapies or, for hepatic encephalopathy, other first-line medications used alone or alongside rifaximin. Ask whether a lower-cost option fits your situation.

I tell patients with commercial insurance to enroll in the Xifaxan Savings Card before their first fill, because the price at the counter can be a shock without it. For patients on Medicare, I remind them that the copay card won't work, but the 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap and the Bausch Health assistance program can make long-term therapy for hepatic encephalopathy manageable. If a prior authorization is denied, an appeal from the prescriber documenting the diagnosis often succeeds.

Xifaxan cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Xifaxan cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per prescription with the Xifaxan Savings Card. The card is for commercial insurance only — not Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic Xifaxan?

A generic rifaximin is FDA-approved but not widely available. Ask your pharmacist whether it can be obtained. Xifaxan is a small-molecule antibiotic, so no biosimilar applies.

How much is Xifaxan without insurance?

The approximate list price is ~$2,000–4,000/mo (550 mg). Discount cards may reduce the cash price, and uninsured patients can apply to the Bausch Health Patient Assistance Program.

Does Medicare cover Xifaxan?

Many Part D plans cover Xifaxan, often with prior authorization, and cost varies by plan. Xifaxan is not a Medicare-negotiated drug. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100, and the manufacturer copay card cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Xifaxan Savings Card?

Enroll online at https://xifaxan.copaysavingsprogram.com, then present the card at your pharmacy with your prescription. You must have commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible.
