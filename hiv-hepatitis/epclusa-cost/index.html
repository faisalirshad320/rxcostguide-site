Epclusa cost, coupon & authorized generic

Epclusa (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir) is a pan-genotypic hepatitis C cure taken as a 12-week course — and it has a much cheaper authorized generic.

Manufacturer copay cardPatient assistance

The short answer

Branded Epclusa lists around $25,000 for a 12-week course, but an authorized generic (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir) is available at a fraction of that — often a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars for the full course. Commercially insured patients can use the copay card; assistance programs help others.

Epclusa cost with Medicare

Epclusa is not on the Medicare negotiated-price list. Part D members pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 annual cap — and because treatment is a one-time course, that cap often covers the whole cure. The authorized generic may be priced lower on a Part D plan. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Epclusa

Gilead's Support Path provides Epclusa free to uninsured, income-eligible patients and offers copay assistance for the commercially insured.

Cash-pay strategies for Epclusa

The authorized generic is the key: ask your pharmacist or Gilead Support Path about the authorized generic sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, which costs a fraction of the brand. For a curative course, this can be the difference between $25,000 and a few hundred dollars.

Cheaper alternatives to Epclusa

Mavyret (glecaprevir/pibrentasvir) is the main alternative pan-genotypic hepatitis C cure, often as an 8-week course. Both are highly effective; the choice depends on your genotype, prior treatment and any cirrhosis. See our Mavyret page.

Hepatitis C is one of modern medicine's success stories — an actual cure. The tragedy would be someone forgoing it over a list price that almost never applies. Between the authorized generic and Gilead's Support Path, the real cost is a tiny fraction of the sticker. I tell patients: the $25,000 number is not your number.

Epclusa cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Epclusa really cost?

The brand lists around $25,000 for a 12-week course, but the authorized generic costs a fraction of that, and assistance programs bring it to $0 for many. Very few people pay the list price.

Is there a generic for Epclusa?

Yes — Gilead offers an authorized generic of sofosbuvir/velpatasvir at a much lower price than the brand. Ask your pharmacist or Gilead Support Path.

How do I get Epclusa for free?

Uninsured, income-eligible patients can get it free through Gilead's Support Path. Commercially insured patients can use the copay card; compare it against the authorized generic.

Does Medicare cover Epclusa?

Yes, under Part D. Because it's a one-time course, the $2,100 annual cap often covers the whole cure. The authorized generic may be cheaper on your plan.

How long is Epclusa treatment?

Usually a 12-week course. Because it's curative, the cost is one-time rather than an ongoing monthly bill.
