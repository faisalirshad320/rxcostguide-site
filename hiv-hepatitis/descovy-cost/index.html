Descovy cost, copay card & PrEP coverage

Descovy (emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide, F/TAF) is used both as part of HIV treatment and for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) — and for PrEP, you often should not be paying anything at all.

The short answer

Descovy lists around $2,200 a month. But if you take it for PrEP, the ACA requires most commercial plans to cover PrEP and its associated visits at $0, so you often pay nothing. Commercially insured patients can also use the Gilead Advancing Access copay card ($0); uninsured patients have Ready, Set, PrEP and ADAP. There is no generic Descovy (though generic F/TDF exists for PrEP).

Descovy’s cost story depends entirely on why you take it. For PrEP, a 2021 federal rule means most insured patients should pay $0 — including for the required lab visits — and many still get wrongly billed. For HIV treatment, Descovy is one component of a regimen, covered through the routes below.

Descovy cost with Medicare

Descovy has no Medicare-negotiated price. Medicare now covers PrEP at $0 as a preventive service (via Part B as of 2025), including the drug and visits. For HIV treatment, Part D plans cover Descovy subject to formulary, under the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket cap. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Descovy

Gilead provides Descovy free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through Advancing Access. For PrEP, the federal Ready, Set, PrEP program and state ADAPs also provide it at no cost to eligible people.

Gilead Advancing Access / Ready, Set, PrEP

Cash-pay strategies for Descovy

Almost no one should pay cash for Descovy. For PrEP, insist on $0 ACA/Medicare preventive coverage, or use Ready, Set, PrEP (uninsured). For treatment, Gilead Advancing Access and ADAP cover eligible patients. Note that for PrEP, generic emtricitabine/tenofovir DF (the Truvada generic) is a cheaper alternative suitable for many people.

Cheaper alternatives to Descovy

For PrEP, generic F/TDF (Truvada) is a far cheaper option appropriate for most people; Descovy (F/TAF) is preferred in specific situations. Long-acting injectable PrEP (Apretude) is another route. For treatment, Descovy is combined with other agents or replaced by single-tablet regimens like Biktarvy. Clinical choice belongs to your provider.

The single most useful thing I can tell a PrEP patient is that you should not be paying for it. The federal preventive-services rule covers the drug and the monitoring visits at $0 for most insured people, yet wrongful bills are common — usually a coding error on the visit. If you get one, it is almost always worth disputing rather than paying.

Descovy cost — frequently asked questions

Is PrEP with Descovy really free?

For most people with commercial insurance or Medicare, yes — PrEP and its required visits must be covered with no cost-sharing. Uninsured people can use Ready, Set, PrEP or ADAP. Wrongful bills happen and can be disputed.

Is there a generic for Descovy?

Not for Descovy (F/TAF) itself. But for PrEP, generic emtricitabine/tenofovir DF (the Truvada generic) is a cheaper alternative suitable for many people.

How much is Descovy without insurance?

About $2,200 a month at list price, but uninsured people rarely pay that — Gilead Advancing Access, Ready, Set, PrEP, and ADAP provide it free to eligible patients.

Does Medicare cover Descovy for PrEP?

Yes — Medicare covers PrEP at $0 as a preventive service, including the drug and visits. For HIV treatment it is covered under Part D with the $2,100 cap.

Can I use the Gilead copay card on Medicare?

No — it is for commercial insurance only. Medicare patients use preventive coverage (PrEP) or their Part D plan (treatment).
