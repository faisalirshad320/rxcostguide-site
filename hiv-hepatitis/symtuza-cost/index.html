Symtuza cost, coupon & copay card

Symtuza (darunavir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide) is a once-daily single-tablet complete regimen for treating HIV-1 infection in eligible adults and adolescents.

The short answer

Symtuza has a list price of about ~$4,615/mo, but most people with commercial insurance pay $0 per fill with the Symtuza withMe Savings Program (benefit up to $12,500/yr). On Medicare Part D, the copay card can't be used, but your out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. Uninsured or underinsured patients can apply to the Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation.

Symtuza combines four antiretroviral medicines in one tablet taken once daily with food, giving a complete HIV-1 regimen without a separate pill burden. Because it's a brand-only combination product taken indefinitely, the monthly list price is high, and what you actually pay depends mostly on your insurance type, plan formulary tier, and whether you use manufacturer savings or assistance programs.

The Symtuza copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay $0 per fill with the Symtuza withMe Savings Program.

The Symtuza withMe Savings Program covers out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients with commercial or private insurance, up to $12,500/yr. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government-funded insurance. Enroll online through Johnson & Johnson's withMe site, then present the card details at your pharmacy with each fill.

Symtuza cost with Medicare

Symtuza is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan, but in 2026 your total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 for the year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the withMe Savings Program can't be used; ask your plan about spreading costs across the year, and check whether you qualify for Extra Help or the J&J Patient Assistance Foundation.

Patient assistance for Symtuza

The Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation may provide Symtuza at no cost to eligible patients who are uninsured or underinsured and meet income requirements. Your HIV care team or a social worker can help you complete the application and gather proof of income.

Cash-pay strategies for Symtuza

Without insurance, Symtuza's list price is about ~$4,615/mo, and pharmacy discount cards typically reduce brand-only HIV regimens only modestly. Applying to the Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation is usually the most effective route, and your state AIDS Drug Assistance Program (ADAP) may also cover HIV medicines. Ask your clinician whether a lower-cost regimen that suits your treatment history is an option.

Cheaper alternatives to Symtuza

Other single-tablet HIV-1 regimens and multi-pill combinations exist, and some may be less expensive depending on your formulary; the right choice depends on your resistance profile, kidney function, drug interactions, and prior treatment, so any switch should be made with your HIV clinician.

I tell patients to enroll in the withMe Savings Program before their first fill so cost never becomes a reason to miss doses, since consistent daily adherence is what keeps HIV suppressed. Because cobicistat boosts levels of many other medicines, I also remind them to share every prescription, over-the-counter product, and supplement with their pharmacist. If insurance changes, especially a move to Medicare, call the pharmacy and care team right away, because the copay card stops applying.

Symtuza cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Symtuza cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, eligible patients may pay $0 per fill with the Symtuza withMe Savings Program, with a program benefit of up to $12,500/yr.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Symtuza?

No. There is no generic version of the Symtuza combination tablet, and biosimilars don't apply because Symtuza is a small-molecule drug, not a biologic.

How much does Symtuza cost without insurance?

Symtuza's list price is about ~$4,615/mo. Uninsured patients should apply to the Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation, which may provide the drug at no cost to those who qualify.

Does Medicare cover Symtuza, and can I use the copay card?

Many Medicare Part D plans cover Symtuza, with cost-sharing that varies by plan, and 2026 out-of-pocket Part D costs are capped at $2,100. The withMe copay card can't be used with Medicare or Medicaid. Symtuza does not have a Medicare-negotiated price.

How do I enroll in the Symtuza withMe Savings Program?

Enroll online at https://www.jnjwithme.com/patient/symtuza/cost-support/. You must have commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible.
