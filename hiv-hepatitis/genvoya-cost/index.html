Genvoya cost, copay card & assistance

Genvoya is a four-in-one, once-daily single-tablet HIV regimen (elvitegravir, cobicistat, emtricitabine and tenofovir alafenamide).

Genvoya cost with Medicare

Genvoya has no Medicare-negotiated price. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their Part D plan cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket maximum. HIV drugs are a protected class on Part D, so coverage is broad. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Genvoya

Gilead provides Genvoya free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through Advancing Access. State ADAPs also cover it for eligible people.

Gilead Advancing Access / ADAP

Cash-pay strategies for Genvoya

Almost no one should pay cash for Genvoya. Commercially insured patients use the Gilead card ($0); uninsured patients use ADAP or Gilead assistance. Take Genvoya with food, as its absorption depends on it. Newer regimens (Biktarvy, Dovato) may be preferred clinically; discuss any change with your provider.

Cheaper alternatives to Genvoya

Newer single-tablet regimens like Biktarvy and the two-drug Dovato are common alternatives, often with simpler interactions (no cobicistat booster). Which regimen fits is a specialist decision based on resistance and other medicines you take.

Genvoya uses a cobicistat "booster," which is clinically fine but interacts with a lot of other medicines — so when I see cost or tolerability questions about it, the conversation often widens into drug interactions. Many patients on older boosted regimens are eventually moved to unboosted ones like Biktarvy; that is a clinical call, but it can also simplify life considerably.

Genvoya cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Genvoya with insurance?

Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 with the Gilead Advancing Access card. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.

Is there a generic for Genvoya?

No — it is brand-only. Cost is managed through the copay card (commercial), ADAP and Gilead assistance (uninsured), or Part D (Medicare).

What does Genvoya cost without insurance?

About $3,900 a month at list price, but ADAP and Gilead assistance provide it free to eligible uninsured patients.

Why must Genvoya be taken with food?

Food improves absorption of the regimen, so it should be taken with a meal. Consistent dosing with food helps maintain effective drug levels.

Is Genvoya still a first-choice regimen?

Newer regimens like Biktarvy and Dovato are often preferred now, partly because Genvoya’s cobicistat booster interacts with many drugs. Any change is a specialist decision.
