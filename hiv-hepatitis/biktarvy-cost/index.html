Biktarvy cost, coupon & copay card

Biktarvy is a once-daily single-tablet regimen for HIV-1 — one of the most widely prescribed HIV treatments.

The short answer

Biktarvy costs about $4,000 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 with the Biktarvy copay card. Uninsured and low-income patients can access it through Gilead Advancing Access, Ryan White or state ADAP. Medicare patients are protected by the $2,100 Part D cap.

Biktarvy cost with Medicare

Biktarvy is not on the current Medicare negotiated-price list. Part D members pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 annual cap. Medicare patients may also qualify for state ADAP help with out-of-pocket costs. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Biktarvy

Gilead Advancing Access provides Biktarvy free to uninsured, income-eligible patients, and connects patients to the Ryan White program and state AIDS Drug Assistance Programs (ADAP).

Gilead Advancing Access / Ryan White / ADAP

Cash-pay strategies for Biktarvy

No one should pay the ~$4,000 list price if it can be avoided. Uninsured patients should contact Gilead Advancing Access and ask about Ryan White and ADAP eligibility in their state — these government programs are designed exactly for this.

Cheaper alternatives to Biktarvy

Other single-tablet HIV regimens (Dovato, Triumeq and others) may be alternatives depending on your treatment history and resistance profile. HIV regimen choice is a specialist decision; cost assistance is similar across the major options.

HIV care has the strongest safety net of any category on this site — between manufacturer programs, Ryan White and ADAP, cost should almost never be a barrier to staying on treatment. If someone tells me they're rationing HIV medication over cost, the fix is connecting them to Advancing Access or ADAP, not changing the drug. Continuity of treatment matters too much to interrupt.

Biktarvy cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Biktarvy without insurance?

About $4,000 a month at list price, but uninsured patients rarely pay that — Gilead Advancing Access, Ryan White and state ADAP programs provide it free or low-cost to those who qualify.

How do I get Biktarvy for $0?

With commercial insurance, the Biktarvy copay card can bring you to as little as $0. Uninsured or low-income patients can access it free through Advancing Access, Ryan White or ADAP.

What is ADAP?

The AIDS Drug Assistance Program is a state-run, federally funded program that provides HIV medication to low-income, uninsured, and underinsured people. Eligibility and covered drugs vary by state.

Does Medicare cover Biktarvy?

Yes, under Part D. You pay plan cost-share up to the $2,100 annual cap, and may qualify for additional ADAP help. Copay cards are not usable on Medicare.

Is there a generic for Biktarvy?

Not yet in the US. The copay card, government programs, or the Part D cap are the routes to lower cost.
