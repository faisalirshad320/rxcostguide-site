Eliquis vs Xarelto: cost, coverage & which is right for you

The two most-prescribed Factor Xa blood thinners, compared on what actually drives your out-of-pocket cost in 2026.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

At list price the two are close — Eliquis about $600/month, Xarelto about $550/month. On Medicare, Xarelto's 2026 negotiated price (~$197) is a little lower than Eliquis's (~$231). For commercially insured patients, both copay cards can reach near $0, so cost rarely decides it — dosing frequency and your doctor's judgment do.

When Eliquis tends to be the choice

Eliquis is often preferred when twice-daily dosing is acceptable and when clinicians want the specific trial evidence behind apixaban in atrial fibrillation and certain kidney-function situations. Its 2026 negotiated price makes it very affordable on Medicare.

When Xarelto tends to be the choice

Xarelto's once-daily dosing can help adherence for patients who struggle with twice-daily regimens. Its 2026 negotiated price is slightly lower than Eliquis's, and its copay card can reach $0 for commercial patients. Some doses must be taken with food.

Which is cheaper for you?

It depends entirely on your insurance. Medicare: Xarelto is marginally cheaper on the negotiated price. Commercial insurance: both reach near $0 with the copay card — effectively a tie. Uninsured: both are ~$550–600 list; apply to the manufacturer's patient-assistance program. Never switch drugs for a small cost difference without your doctor — the clinical fit matters far more than the price gap here.

FAQ

Is Eliquis or Xarelto safer?

Both are well-established Factor Xa inhibitors with strong safety records. Differences in bleeding risk and suitability depend on individual factors like kidney function and other medications, which is why the choice is your doctor's. This page compares cost, not clinical safety.

Which is cheaper on Medicare in 2026?

Xarelto's negotiated price (~$197) is slightly lower than Eliquis's (~$231) per 30-day supply. Both are far below their old list prices.

Can I switch from one to the other to save money?

Only with your prescriber's agreement. The cost difference is small, and the clinical reasons for choosing one over the other usually outweigh it.
