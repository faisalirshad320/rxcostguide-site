Eliquis cost, coupon & copay card

Eliquis (apixaban) is the most-prescribed anticoagulant in the United States — and one of the first drugs to get a Medicare-negotiated price in 2026.

The short answer

Eliquis costs about $600 a month (roughly $7,100 a year) at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $10 a month with the Eliquis Co-pay Card. From 1 January 2026 the Medicare-negotiated price is about $231 per 30-day supply, applied automatically at the pharmacy for Part D members.

Eliquis prevents strokes in atrial fibrillation and treats and prevents blood clots. Because it is taken indefinitely, the annual cost is what matters. What you actually pay depends almost entirely on your insurance type — commercial plan, Medicare, or uninsured — and each has a different cheapest route, laid out below.

The Eliquis copay card

The Eliquis Co-pay Card can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $10 per month.

The card is for people with commercial (private) insurance only — it cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or any government plan, which is the most common reason it is declined. There is an annual limit on total savings, so 90-day fills stretch the card further than three monthly ones. Enroll online in a few minutes; the card activates immediately.

Eliquis cost with Medicare

Eliquis was one of the first ten drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. From 1 January 2026 the Maximum Fair Price is about $231 per 30-day supply, down from a list price near $521 — a cut of more than half. You do not apply for it; it becomes the price Part D uses at the counter. If your pharmacy appears to charge against the old, higher figure, raise it with them. Remember the Part D out-of-pocket cap is $2,100 for 2026.

Patient assistance for Eliquis

Bristol Myers Squibb runs a patient-assistance program for uninsured patients who meet income limits, which can provide Eliquis at no cost. The application asks for proof of income and residency and is usually decided within a couple of weeks.

Cash-pay strategies for Eliquis

If you are paying cash, always compare a discount-card price (GoodRx, SingleCare) against your pharmacy's cash price — they frequently beat it, bringing Eliquis closer to $550. Ask for a 90-day supply, which lowers per-fill dispensing costs. A generic apixaban has begun to reach the US market and, as it becomes widely stocked, will be the cheapest route of all — ask your pharmacist whether generic apixaban is available to you yet.

Cheaper alternatives to Eliquis

Xarelto (rivaroxaban) is the closest alternative, taken once daily rather than twice; its cost profile is similar and it also has a 2026 Medicare-negotiated price. Warfarin is far cheaper (a few dollars a month) but requires regular blood-test monitoring, which Eliquis does not. Any switch is a clinical decision for your prescriber — see our Eliquis vs Xarelto comparison.

In the hospital blood bank and pathology service, the anticoagulant a patient is on shapes how we handle their sample and any urgent reversal. What I tell patients on the cost side is simple: your insurance *type* decides your cheapest path far more than any coupon. A Medicare patient and a commercially insured patient standing at the same pharmacy counter should be doing completely different things — the Medicare patient relies on the negotiated price, the commercial patient on the copay card. Mixing those up is where people overpay.

Eliquis cost — frequently asked questions

Why is Eliquis so expensive without insurance?

Eliquis is still a branded drug under patent protection for most of the US market, so there is little price competition. List price is about $600 a month. That is beginning to change as generic apixaban reaches pharmacies, which will lower cash prices over time.

How do seniors on Medicare afford Eliquis in 2026?

From 1 January 2026, Medicare's negotiated price of about $231 per 30-day supply applies automatically at the pharmacy. Combined with the $2,100 annual Part D out-of-pocket cap, most Medicare patients now have a predictable, much lower yearly cost. The manufacturer copay card cannot be used with Medicare.

Can I use the Eliquis copay card with Medicare?

No. By law, manufacturer copay cards cannot be used by anyone with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Medicare patients rely on the negotiated price and the Part D cap instead.

Is there a generic for Eliquis?

Generic apixaban has begun entering the US market. Availability varies by pharmacy and region; ask your pharmacist whether generic apixaban is in stock for you, as it is the cheapest option once available.

Does a 90-day supply of Eliquis save money?

Usually yes. Copay-card savings are capped per year, and 90-day fills mean fewer dispensing events and fewer chances for a coverage lapse, so they typically stretch both your card and your out-of-pocket budget further.
