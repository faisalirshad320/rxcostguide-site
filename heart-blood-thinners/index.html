What heart and blood-thinner medications cost, and how to pay less

Anticoagulants and cardiovascular drugs are taken indefinitely, so the annual figure matters more than the monthly one. Two of the most-prescribed — Eliquis and Xarelto — now have Medicare-negotiated prices that took effect on 1 January 2026.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Branded blood thinners and heart drugs run roughly $450 to $650 a month at list price — modest per month, but $5,000 to $8,000 a year for medication most people take for life.

The biggest change in this category is Medicare price negotiation: Eliquis, Xarelto and Entresto all have negotiated prices effective 1 January 2026, applied automatically at the pharmacy counter for Part D patients. If you have commercial insurance instead, the manufacturer copay card is almost always the cheapest route.

What Medicare negotiation changed here

Eliquis, Xarelto and Entresto were among the first ten drugs selected for the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program. The negotiated figure — the Maximum Fair Price — is not a discount you apply for; it becomes what Part D pays at the counter from 1 January 2026. Alongside it, the Part D out-of-pocket cap is $2,100 for 2026, which for most people on one or two cardiac drugs is now the number that decides annual spending.

Commercial insurance: use the copay card

If your coverage is commercial (through an employer or the marketplace, not Medicare or Medicaid), every branded drug on this page has a manufacturer copay card that can bring you to $0–$10 a month. These cards are barred by law from Medicare and Medicaid patients — that restriction is the single most common reason a card is rejected at the pharmacy.
