Xarelto cost, coupon & copay card

Xarelto (rivaroxaban) is a once-daily Factor Xa inhibitor and, like Eliquis, one of the first drugs to receive a Medicare-negotiated price in 2026.

The short answer

Xarelto costs about $550 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 with the Janssen CarePath copay card. From 1 January 2026 the Medicare-negotiated price is about $197 per 30-day supply, applied automatically for Part D members.

Xarelto prevents strokes in atrial fibrillation and treats and prevents blood clots, taken once daily. As with all long-term anticoagulants, the annual cost is what counts, and — like Eliquis — the cheapest route depends entirely on whether you have commercial insurance, Medicare, or no coverage.

The Xarelto copay card

The Janssen CarePath Savings Program can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $0 per month.

Like all manufacturer cards it is for commercial insurance only — never Medicare or Medicaid. There is an annual maximum on savings, so 90-day fills use the card more efficiently. Enrollment is quick and the card works at the pharmacy immediately.

Xarelto cost with Medicare

Xarelto was among the first ten drugs selected for Medicare negotiation. From 1 January 2026 its Maximum Fair Price is about $197 per 30-day supply, down from roughly $517 — a cut of more than 60%. The price applies automatically at the pharmacy for Part D members; there is nothing to apply for. The 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap of $2,100 also applies.

Patient assistance for Xarelto

J&J's patient-assistance foundation can provide Xarelto free to uninsured patients who meet income requirements. Applications require proof of income and are typically processed within a couple of weeks.

Cash-pay strategies for Xarelto

For cash payers, compare GoodRx and SingleCare prices against the pharmacy's own cash price — discount cards frequently bring Xarelto below list. Request a 90-day supply to reduce per-fill costs. There is no US generic rivaroxaban widely available yet, so discount cards remain the main cash lever.

Cheaper alternatives to Xarelto

Eliquis (apixaban) is the closest alternative — twice daily instead of once, with a similar cost profile and its own 2026 negotiated price. Warfarin is far cheaper but needs regular INR monitoring. See our Eliquis vs Xarelto comparison for the trade-offs.

Once-daily dosing is why some patients prefer Xarelto to Eliquis, and adherence genuinely matters with anticoagulants — a missed dose is not trivial. On cost, my advice is identical to what I give Eliquis patients: confirm your insurance type first, then take the matching route. Commercial patients who never enroll in the CarePath card are leaving hundreds of dollars a year on the table.

Xarelto cost — frequently asked questions

Is Xarelto cheaper than Eliquis?

At list price they are similar (Xarelto ~$550, Eliquis ~$600). On Medicare, Xarelto's 2026 negotiated price (~$197) is slightly lower than Eliquis's (~$231). For commercially insured patients, both copay cards can reach near $0, so the practical difference is small.

How much is Xarelto with Medicare in 2026?

From 1 January 2026 the Medicare-negotiated price is about $197 per 30-day supply, applied automatically at the pharmacy. Your annual Part D out-of-pocket spending is also capped at $2,100.

Can I use the Xarelto copay card on Medicare?

No. Manufacturer copay cards, including Janssen CarePath, cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. Medicare patients rely on the negotiated price and Part D cap.

Is there a generic version of Xarelto?

A widely stocked US generic rivaroxaban is not yet available. Until it is, discount cards and — for eligible patients — the copay card or patient assistance are the main ways to lower cost.

What is the cheapest way to get Xarelto without insurance?

Compare GoodRx/SingleCare against the pharmacy cash price, ask for a 90-day supply, and if you are income-eligible apply to the J&J Patient Assistance Foundation, which can provide it at no cost.
