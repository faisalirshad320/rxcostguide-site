Repatha cost, coupon & copay card

Repatha (evolocumab) is a PCSK9 inhibitor that lowers LDL cholesterol when statins are not enough, given as a self-injection.

Manufacturer copay cardPatient assistance

The short answer

Repatha costs about $650 a month (roughly $7,800 a year) at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $5 a month with the Repatha copay card. Repatha does not currently have a Medicare-negotiated price, so Medicare patients pay their plan's cost-share up to the $2,100 Part D cap.

Repatha is used when statins alone cannot bring LDL cholesterol low enough, or when statins are not tolerated. It is a long-term therapy given by self-injection every two weeks (or monthly at a higher dose). As with most branded injectables, your insurance type sets the cheapest path.

The Repatha copay card

The RepathaReady copay card can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $5 per month.

Commercial insurance only — it cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. There is an annual maximum. Because Repatha is dispensed in 1- or 3-month supplies, a 3-month fill often uses the card most efficiently.

Repatha cost with Medicare

Repatha is not on the current Medicare negotiated-price list, so Part D members pay their plan's cost-share. The 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap of $2,100 still applies, which for many patients is the figure that determines their annual spend. Manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Repatha

Amgen provides Repatha at no cost to uninsured patients who meet income requirements through its Safety Net Foundation. Amgen SupportPlus also helps commercially insured patients with benefits investigation and prior authorization.

Amgen SupportPlus / Safety Net Foundation

Cash-pay strategies for Repatha

For cash payers, discount cards can reduce the list price somewhat, but PCSK9 inhibitors remain expensive out of pocket. If you are uninsured and income-eligible, the Amgen Safety Net Foundation is the most reliable route to $0. A lower-cost PCSK9 option (see alternatives) may also be worth discussing with your doctor.

Cheaper alternatives to Repatha

Praluent (alirocumab) is a directly comparable PCSK9 inhibitor with a similar cost and its own copay card. A newer twice-yearly injectable, inclisiran (Leqvio), works on the same pathway with far fewer doses. Ezetimibe is an inexpensive oral add-on to statins. Which is appropriate is a clinical decision for your prescriber.

PCSK9 inhibitors are genuinely effective for patients who cannot get their LDL down on statins alone — but they are also a classic case where prior authorization trips people up. The most common reason I see coverage denied is missing documentation that statins were tried first. If your prescription stalls, that paperwork is usually the fix, not the price.

Repatha cost — frequently asked questions

What does Repatha cost without insurance?

About $650 per month, or roughly $7,800 a year, at list price for the standard dose. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can often get it free through the Amgen Safety Net Foundation.

How do I get Repatha cheaper?

If you have commercial insurance, the RepathaReady copay card can bring you to about $5 a month. If you are on Medicare, rely on your Part D coverage and the $2,100 annual cap. If uninsured, apply to Amgen's Safety Net Foundation.

Is Repatha covered by Medicare?

Yes, Repatha is generally covered under Medicare Part D, though it often requires prior authorization. It does not have a Medicare-negotiated price, so you pay your plan's cost-share up to the $2,100 Part D out-of-pocket cap.

What is the Repatha cash price?

The cash/list price is about $650 per month. Discount cards can lower this somewhat, but the manufacturer copay card (commercial insurance) or Amgen patient assistance (uninsured) are usually far cheaper routes.

Can I use the Repatha copay card with Medicare?

No. The RepathaReady card is for commercial insurance only and cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.
