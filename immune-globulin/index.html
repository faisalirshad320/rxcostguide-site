What immune globulin (IVIG/SCIG) costs, and how to pay less

Immune globulin replacement for primary immunodeficiency and CIDP is dosed by body weight, so the total cost is large even though the per-gram price looks modest.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-13
The short answer

Immune globulin runs roughly $180 to $260 per gram, and because dosing is weight-based the monthly total is often thousands of dollars. Commercially insured patients can usually get most or all of their copay covered by the manufacturer program (up to ~$20,000/yr). Medicare patients pay Part B or Part D cost-share; uninsured patients can apply for patient assistance.

Why the total cost is so high

The per-gram price is only part of the story — immune globulin is dosed by body weight, so a typical month can require many grams. Ask whether your product is billed under Part B (often clinic-infused IVIG) or Part D/pharmacy (often home SCIG), because that changes your cost-share and which assistance applies.
