Hizentra cost, coupon & copay card

Hizentra (immune globulin subcutaneous) is a plasma-derived antibody replacement therapy from CSL Behring for people with primary immunodeficiency or chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP).

The short answer

Hizentra's list price is ~$256 per gram (total varies by dose/weight), so the total cost depends on your prescribed dose and body weight. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 out-of-pocket with the Hizentra Co-Pay Assistance Program. On Medicare, coverage may fall under Part B or Part D depending on how it is supplied; if it is billed under Part D, your out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026.

Hizentra is infused under the skin at home, usually on a weekly or every-other-week schedule, using a small infusion pump. Because it is dosed by body weight and priced per gram, the total cost scales with your dose and is high at list price. What you actually pay depends heavily on your insurance type, your plan's specialty-drug tier, and whether you qualify for manufacturer assistance.

The Hizentra copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 out-of-pocket with the Hizentra Co-Pay Assistance Program.

The Hizentra Co-Pay Assistance Program helps cover out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients with commercial (private or employer) insurance. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. An annual program maximum applies, so confirm the current terms when you enroll.

Hizentra cost with Medicare

Hizentra is not on the list of drugs with a Medicare negotiated price. Depending on how it is supplied, Medicare may cover it under Part B or Part D. If it is covered under Part D, your out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026. Manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare, but you may still apply for CSL Behring patient assistance.

Patient assistance for Hizentra

Hizentra Connect and CSL Behring's US support programs help with benefits verification, insurance navigation, and financial assistance for eligible patients, including those who are uninsured, underinsured, or unable to use the copay program. Visit the CSL Behring support page to review options and apply.

Cash-pay strategies for Hizentra

Without insurance, Hizentra is costly at ~$256 per gram (total varies by dose/weight), and pharmacy discount cards usually offer little savings on specialty biologics like this. Your best option is to contact Hizentra Connect / CSL Behring about patient assistance. Your clinician may also review whether another immune globulin product or setting is more affordable under your circumstances.

Cheaper alternatives to Hizentra

There is no generic or biosimilar version of Hizentra. Other subcutaneous (SCIG) and intravenous (IVIG) immune globulin products exist, and your clinician can advise whether switching makes sense, since products are not automatically interchangeable.

I tell patients to start the insurance and assistance paperwork as soon as Hizentra is prescribed, because specialty pharmacy setup and prior authorization can take time. I also remind them that dose changes based on weight or clinical response change the total cost, so they should recheck their coverage after any adjustment. If you are on Medicare, ask whether your therapy is billed under Part B or Part D, because that determines how your costs are capped.

Hizentra cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Hizentra cost with the copay card?

Eligible patients with commercial insurance may pay as little as $0 out-of-pocket with the Hizentra Co-Pay Assistance Program. An annual program maximum applies per prescription per year.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Hizentra?

No. Hizentra has no generic or biosimilar version. Other immune globulin brands exist, but they are not automatically interchangeable, so ask your clinician before switching.

How much is Hizentra without insurance?

The list price is ~$256 per gram (total varies by dose/weight). Your total depends on your weight-based dose and infusion schedule. Uninsured patients should contact Hizentra Connect / CSL Behring about patient assistance.

Does Medicare cover Hizentra, and can I use the copay card?

Medicare may cover Hizentra under Part B or Part D, depending on how it is supplied. If it is covered under Part D, out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. The copay card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid, but you can still apply for CSL Behring patient assistance.

How do I enroll in the Hizentra copay program?

Enroll through the official Hizentra financial assistance page at https://www.hizentra.com/pi/starting-hizentra/patient-services-and-financial-assistance/. The program is for commercial insurance only, not Medicare or Medicaid.
