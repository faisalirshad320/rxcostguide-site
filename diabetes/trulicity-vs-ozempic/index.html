Trulicity vs Ozempic: cost, coverage & which is right for you

Two once-weekly GLP-1 injections for type 2 diabetes, compared on what actually drives your out-of-pocket cost in 2026.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

List prices are close — Trulicity about $990/month, Ozempic about $970/month. Both savings cards can reach $25/month or less for commercially insured patients whose plan covers the drug. Neither has a 2026 Medicare-negotiated price, though Ozempic (semaglutide) gets one in 2027. In practice, which your plan places on a better formulary tier decides the cost, not the sticker price.

When Trulicity tends to be the choice

Trulicity’s pen is often cited as the simplest to use — no dose dialing, a hidden needle, once weekly. For patients who find injections stressful, that simplicity matters. Cost-wise it is comparable to Ozempic, so coverage usually decides.

When Ozempic tends to be the choice

Ozempic (semaglutide) tends to produce slightly greater A1c and weight reduction in trials, and it is the same molecule as Wegovy. From 2027 it also gains a Medicare-negotiated price. If your plan prefers it, or you may later want the weight-loss indication, it can be the pick.

Which is cheaper for you?

Commercial insurance: whichever your plan covers on the better tier — run both cards at the pharmacy. Medicare: similar today; Ozempic gains a negotiated price in 2027. Uninsured: similar list prices — apply to Lilly Cares (Trulicity) or NovoCare (Ozempic). Never switch GLP-1s for a small price gap without your prescriber.

FAQ

Is Trulicity or Ozempic cheaper?

List prices are nearly identical (~$990 vs ~$970), and both cards reach ~$25/month for covered commercial patients. The cheaper one is whichever your specific plan places on a better tier.

Can I switch between Trulicity and Ozempic?

Only your prescriber should switch you. They are different molecules at different doses and not automatically interchangeable, though both are once-weekly GLP-1s.

Does either have a Medicare-negotiated price?

Not in 2026. Ozempic (semaglutide) gains a negotiated price effective 2027; Trulicity (dulaglutide) has none scheduled.
