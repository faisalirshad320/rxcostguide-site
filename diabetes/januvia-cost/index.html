Januvia cost, coupon & generic options

Januvia (sitagliptin) is a DPP-4 inhibitor for type 2 diabetes — one of the first ten Medicare-negotiated drugs, with generic sitagliptin beginning to arrive.

The short answer

Branded Januvia costs about $620 a month at list price. From 1 January 2026 the Medicare-negotiated price is about $113 per 30-day supply. Generic sitagliptin is beginning to reach pharmacies and will be the cheapest route once widely stocked.

Januvia cost with Medicare

Januvia was one of the first ten Medicare-negotiated drugs. From 1 January 2026 its Maximum Fair Price is about $113 per 30-day supply, down from roughly $527 — applied automatically for Part D members, with the $2,100 annual cap. Where generic sitagliptin is stocked, a Part D plan may price it even lower.

Patient assistance for Januvia

Merck provides Januvia free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through its patient-assistance program.

Merck Patient Assistance Program

Cash-pay strategies for Januvia

The biggest lever is the generic: ask your pharmacist for generic sitagliptin and price it with GoodRx or SingleCare. Request a 90-day supply. Older diabetes drugs like metformin are far cheaper still, if clinically appropriate for you.

Cheaper alternatives to Januvia

Other DPP-4 inhibitors (Tradjenta) and SGLT2 inhibitors (Jardiance, Farxiga) are alternatives with different cost profiles. Metformin remains the inexpensive first-line option. Any switch is a clinical decision.

Januvia sits at an interesting moment — a negotiated Medicare price *and* an emerging generic at the same time. For most patients the generic will eventually win on price, but until it's reliably stocked, the negotiated price makes the brand very manageable on Medicare. Ask the pharmacist which is actually cheaper for you today; the answer is changing month to month.

Januvia cost — frequently asked questions

Is there a generic for Januvia?

Generic sitagliptin is beginning to reach the US market. Where stocked, it is usually the cheapest option — ask your pharmacist.

How much is Januvia on Medicare in 2026?

About $113 per 30-day supply — the negotiated price, applied automatically. The $2,100 Part D cap also applies.

Can I use the Januvia savings card on Medicare?

No — commercial insurance only. Medicare patients use the negotiated price and Part D cap.

What is the cheapest DPP-4 inhibitor?

Generic sitagliptin, where available, is typically the cheapest DPP-4 option. Compare it against your plan and discount-card prices.

What does Januvia cost without insurance?

Branded Januvia is about $620 a month; generic sitagliptin is much less. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can apply to Merck's patient-assistance program for free brand.
