Trulicity cost, coupon & savings card

Trulicity (dulaglutide) is a once-weekly GLP-1 injection for type 2 diabetes — one of the most prescribed drugs in its class.

Manufacturer copay cardPatient assistance

The short answer

Trulicity costs about $990 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $25 a month with the Trulicity savings card (if their plan covers it). There is no generic. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap; the uninsured can apply to Lilly Cares.

Trulicity is taken once a week and indefinitely, so annual cost is what matters. As with most GLP-1 drugs, whether your commercial plan covers it is the single biggest factor in what you actually pay.

The Trulicity copay card

The Trulicity savings card can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $25 per month.

If your commercial plan covers Trulicity, the card can bring you to about $25 a month. Commercial insurance only — never Medicare or Medicaid. A monthly and annual maximum applies, so a 90-day prescription uses the card efficiently.

Trulicity cost with Medicare

Trulicity (dulaglutide) does not have a Medicare-negotiated price. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their Part D plan cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket maximum. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Trulicity

Lilly Cares provides Trulicity free to uninsured, income-eligible patients. Proof of income and a prescription are required.

Lilly Cares Foundation

Cash-pay strategies for Trulicity

There is no generic dulaglutide, so cash payers should compare discount-card prices and ask about a 90-day supply. If income-eligible and uninsured, Lilly Cares can provide it free. Other GLP-1 options with their own savings programs may be cheaper depending on your coverage.

Cheaper alternatives to Trulicity

Ozempic (semaglutide) is the main once-weekly GLP-1 alternative for diabetes; Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is a newer dual agonist. See our Trulicity vs Ozempic comparison. Older, cheaper diabetes drugs like metformin work differently; any switch is clinical.

Trulicity’s pen is genuinely simple, which patients value, but the coverage lottery is real: the same drug can be $25 or several hundred dollars depending purely on whether a plan lists it. Before assuming a price, I always tell patients to have the pharmacy run it through their specific plan with the savings card applied — the number often surprises people in both directions.

Trulicity cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Trulicity with the savings card?

As little as $25 a month if your commercial plan covers it. The card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic for Trulicity?

No — dulaglutide is brand-only in the US. The savings card (commercial), your Part D plan (Medicare), or Lilly Cares (uninsured) are the main ways to lower cost.

What does Trulicity cost without insurance?

About $990 a month at list price. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can often get it free through Lilly Cares; otherwise compare discount cards.

Is Trulicity or Ozempic cheaper?

List prices are similar (~$990 vs ~$970). What you pay depends on which your plan covers and places on a better tier — see our Trulicity vs Ozempic comparison.

Can I use the Trulicity card on Medicare?

No — commercial insurance only. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.
