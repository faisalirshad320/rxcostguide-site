Mounjaro cost, coupon & savings card

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is a once-weekly injection for type 2 diabetes and one of the most-searched drug prices in the US.

The short answer

Mounjaro costs about $1,080 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients with coverage can pay as little as $25 a month with the Mounjaro savings card; those whose commercial plan doesn't cover it may pay a capped amount instead. Mounjaro is in the second Medicare negotiation cycle, with a negotiated price effective 2027 — not yet in 2026.

Mounjaro is the diabetes version of tirzepatide; Zepbound is the same molecule approved for weight loss. What you pay depends heavily on whether your commercial plan covers it, and Lilly has introduced lower-cost self-pay options that have shifted the picture.

The Mounjaro copay card

The Mounjaro savings card can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $25 per month.

If your commercial plan covers Mounjaro, the card can bring you to about $25 a month. If your commercial plan does not cover it, the card caps your monthly cost at a higher (but still reduced) figure. Commercial insurance only — never Medicare or Medicaid. Lilly also sells single-dose self-pay vials through LillyDirect at prices well below the pen list price, which can be the cheapest route for cash payers.

Mounjaro cost with Medicare

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is in the second Medicare negotiation cycle, with a negotiated price effective 1 January 2027 — so in 2026 Medicare patients pay their plan cost-share, capped by the $2,100 Part D out-of-pocket maximum. Note that Medicare Part D covers tirzepatide for diabetes, not for weight loss. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Mounjaro

Lilly Cares provides Mounjaro free to uninsured, income-eligible patients. LillyDirect self-pay vials are a separate, lower-cost cash option for those who don't qualify for free assistance.

Cash-pay strategies for Mounjaro

For cash payers, the LillyDirect self-pay vials are usually far cheaper than paying list price for the pens — check current vial pricing on LillyDirect. Compare that against discount-card prices for the pens. If your income qualifies, Lilly Cares can provide it free.

Cheaper alternatives to Mounjaro

Ozempic (semaglutide) is the main once-weekly alternative for diabetes; its Medicare-negotiated price arrives in 2027 too. For weight loss specifically, the same molecule is sold as Zepbound. See our [Mounjaro vs Ozempic comparison](/glp-1-weight-loss/mounjaro-vs-ozempic/).

These GIP/GLP-1 drugs are where I see the most confusion, because the same molecule has a diabetes brand and a weight-loss brand with completely different coverage. Medicare and most plans cover tirzepatide for diabetes (Mounjaro) far more readily than for weight loss (Zepbound). If cost or coverage seems strange, the first question is always: which indication is your prescription actually written for?

Mounjaro cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Mounjaro without insurance?

About $1,080 a month at list price for the pens. Lilly's self-pay single-dose vials through LillyDirect are cheaper, and income-eligible patients may get it free through Lilly Cares.

How much is Mounjaro with the savings card?

As little as $25 a month if your commercial plan covers it. If your commercial plan doesn't cover it, the card caps your cost at a higher but still-reduced amount. Not usable with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is Mounjaro covered by Medicare?

Medicare Part D covers tirzepatide for type 2 diabetes (Mounjaro), subject to your plan. A Medicare-negotiated price takes effect in 2027. Medicare does not cover it for weight loss.

What is the difference between Mounjaro and Zepbound?

They are the same molecule (tirzepatide). Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is approved for weight loss. Coverage and pricing differ by indication.

Are Mounjaro self-pay vials cheaper?

Usually yes. Lilly sells single-dose vials through LillyDirect at prices well below the pen list price, which is often the cheapest cash route.
