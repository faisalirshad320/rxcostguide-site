Kerendia cost, coupon & copay card

Kerendia (finerenone) is a once-daily oral nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist from Bayer used to treat chronic kidney disease associated with type 2 diabetes and heart failure.

The short answer

Kerendia has a list price of about ~$707/mo. With commercial insurance, the Kerendia Savings Card can bring the cost to as little as $0 per monthly prescription, with savings of up to $3,000/yr. On Medicare Part D, the card cannot be used, but your total out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026, and uninsured or underinsured patients can apply to the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation.

Kerendia is taken as a tablet once daily, usually as a long-term therapy alongside other kidney- and heart-protective medications. Because it is a brand-name drug without a generic version, what you pay depends mainly on your insurance type, your plan's formulary tier and deductible, and whether you qualify for the manufacturer savings card or patient assistance.

The Kerendia copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per monthly prescription with the Kerendia Savings Card.

The Kerendia Savings Card is Bayer's copay program for people with commercial (private or employer) insurance. It can lower your out-of-pocket cost for each monthly prescription, with total savings of up to $3,000/yr. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Enroll through the official Kerendia savings and support page and present the card at your pharmacy.

Kerendia cost with Medicare

Kerendia is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price, so your cost depends on your Part D plan's formulary tier, deductible, and coinsurance. In 2026, total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 per year, which limits costs for people on long-term brand-name therapy. Federal rules bar Medicare beneficiaries from using manufacturer copay cards such as the Kerendia Savings Card, but patients with limited income may qualify for Extra Help or for Bayer's patient assistance program.

Patient assistance for Kerendia

The Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation helps eligible uninsured or underinsured patients get Bayer medicines, including Kerendia. Eligibility is based on the foundation's income and insurance criteria; your prescriber's office typically helps complete the application.

Cash-pay strategies for Kerendia

Without insurance, Kerendia's list price is about ~$707/mo, and actual pharmacy cash prices vary. Pharmacy discount cards may lower the price somewhat, but the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation is usually the most meaningful option for uninsured patients who qualify. There is currently no generic finerenone, so ask your prescriber whether a lower-cost alternative is appropriate for your situation.

Cheaper alternatives to Kerendia

Older steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as spironolactone and eplerenone are available as generics, but they are not interchangeable with finerenone and have different evidence and side-effect profiles for these conditions. Any switch should be a decision made with your prescriber, who may also be using other kidney- and heart-protective drugs alongside Kerendia.

I tell patients that Kerendia is a long-term medication, so it's worth sorting out coverage up front—commercially insured patients should enroll in the savings card before the first fill, and Medicare patients should know their 2026 out-of-pocket costs are capped. I also remind them that their blood potassium and kidney function need to be checked before starting and periodically afterward, so they should keep those lab appointments. If cost is ever a barrier, tell your care team rather than skipping doses, because assistance options exist.

Kerendia cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Kerendia cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, the Kerendia Savings Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per monthly prescription, with savings of up to $3,000/yr. It is for commercial insurance only — not Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Kerendia?

No. There is currently no generic finerenone available in the US. Biosimilars apply only to biologic drugs, and Kerendia is a small-molecule tablet, so a biosimilar does not apply.

How much is Kerendia without insurance?

Kerendia's list price is about ~$707/mo. Cash prices vary by pharmacy, and uninsured patients may qualify for help through the Bayer US Patient Assistance Foundation.

Does Medicare cover Kerendia, and can I use the copay card?

Many Medicare Part D plans cover Kerendia, but your cost varies by plan. In 2026, annual out-of-pocket Part D drug costs are capped at $2,100. Medicare patients cannot use the Kerendia Savings Card, and Kerendia does not have a Medicare-negotiated price.

How do I enroll in the Kerendia Savings Card?

Enroll through the official Kerendia savings and support page at https://www.kerendia-us.com/savings-and-support, then give the card information to your pharmacy when you fill your prescription.
