Tresiba cost, coupon & copay card

Tresiba (insulin degludec) is a once-daily, long-acting basal insulin from Novo Nordisk used to control blood sugar in adults and children with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

The short answer

Without insurance, Tresiba varies by form; cash roughly $150–560/mo. With commercial insurance, the Novo Nordisk savings offer can bring it down to as little as $35 per 30-day supply, and on Medicare, the federal $35/month insulin cap applies to covered insulin, with total Part D out-of-pocket costs capped at $2,100 in 2026. A generic insulin degludec is also available and may cost less.

Tresiba provides steady background insulin over a full day and is injected once daily, usually alongside mealtime insulin in type 1 diabetes or with other glucose-lowering medicines in type 2 diabetes. What you pay depends mainly on your insurance type, your plan's formulary placement, and whether you use pens or vials. Because insulin is used every day, small differences in monthly cost add up quickly over a year.

The Tresiba copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $35 per 30-day supply of Tresiba.

The Novo Nordisk Tresiba savings offer (also accessible via MyInsulinRx) lowers your out-of-pocket cost at the pharmacy when you have commercial or private insurance. It is not valid for people with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Enroll online, then present the card information at your pharmacy with each fill.

Tresiba cost with Medicare

Tresiba is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. However, the federal $35/month insulin cap limits what Medicare Part D enrollees pay for covered insulin, and total Part D out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026. Manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare, so Medicare patients should rely on the insulin cap or the Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program.

Patient assistance for Tresiba

The Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program may provide Tresiba at no cost to eligible patients with limited income, including some who are uninsured or on Medicare. Applications typically require proof of income and a prescriber's signature; check the official site for current criteria.

Cash-pay strategies for Tresiba

Without insurance, Tresiba varies by form; cash roughly $150–560/mo, so compare pharmacy prices and discount cards before filling. A generic insulin degludec is available and may be less expensive, and the Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program may help if your income qualifies.

Cheaper alternatives to Tresiba

A generic insulin degludec is available and may lower your cost. Other long-acting basal insulins exist as well; ask your prescriber whether a switch is appropriate, since dosing does not always convert one-to-one.

I tell patients that insulin should never be rationed because of cost; skipping basal insulin can lead to dangerous high blood sugar and, in type 1 diabetes, ketoacidosis. If you are commercially insured, enroll in the savings offer before your first fill, and if you are on Medicare, confirm your plan is applying the $35/month insulin cap. I also encourage patients to ask whether the generic insulin degludec is covered at a lower tier.

Tresiba cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Tresiba cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, the Novo Nordisk savings offer can lower Tresiba to as little as $35 per 30-day supply, with savings of up to $65 off per fill. It is not valid for Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Tresiba?

A generic insulin degludec is available and may cost less than brand-name Tresiba. There is no biosimilar listed. Ask your pharmacist or plan which version is covered at the lowest cost.

How much is Tresiba without insurance?

The cash price varies by form; cash roughly $150–560/mo. Discount cards, the generic insulin degludec, and the Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program can help reduce costs.

How much does Tresiba cost on Medicare?

Medicare Part D enrollees benefit from the federal $35/month insulin cap on covered insulin, and total Part D out-of-pocket costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. Manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Tresiba savings offer?

Commercially insured patients can enroll at https://www.novocare.com/diabetes/products/tresiba/savings-offer.html and present the savings information at the pharmacy. Patients who don't qualify can check the Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program at https://www.novocare.com/diabetes/help-with-costs/pap.html.
