Auvelity cost, coupon & copay card

Auvelity (dextromethorphan-bupropion) is a newer, rapid-acting oral antidepressant for major depressive disorder.

The short answer

Auvelity costs about $1,000 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 a month with the Auvelity savings card. There is no generic. Medicare patients cannot use the card and pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.

Auvelity is a newer brand-only antidepressant, so it is expensive for cash payers but very affordable with the commercial savings card. Its components (dextromethorphan and bupropion) exist separately as cheap generics, but the specific combination and formulation are what is patented.

Auvelity cost with Medicare

Auvelity has no Medicare-negotiated price. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their Part D plan's cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket maximum. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Auvelity

The manufacturer offers a patient-assistance route for uninsured, income-eligible patients. Proof of income and a prescription are required.

Cash-pay strategies for Auvelity

There is no generic for the Auvelity combination, so cash payers should use the savings card if commercially insured, apply for assistance if uninsured, or ask the prescriber whether generic bupropion (alone or with other agents) is a reasonable, far cheaper approach for their situation.

Cheaper alternatives to Auvelity

Generic bupropion is inexpensive and is one of Auvelity’s two components, though the combination and rapid onset are what Auvelity is specifically approved for. Other generic antidepressants are low-cost alternatives. Which is appropriate is a clinical decision for your prescriber.

Auvelity is interesting clinically for its rapid onset, and for a commercially insured patient the $0 card makes trying it easy. For cash or Medicare patients, I’d want the prescriber to be clear on why the branded combination is needed versus generic bupropion, simply because the cost gap is large and the honest answer sometimes is that a generic would serve just as well.

Auvelity cost — frequently asked questions

Is there a generic for Auvelity?

No — the dextromethorphan-bupropion combination is brand-only. Generic bupropion exists on its own and is cheap, but it is not the same approved product.

How much is Auvelity with commercial insurance?

As little as $0 a month with the savings card. It cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

What does Auvelity cost without insurance?

About $1,000 a month at list price. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can apply for free medication through the manufacturer’s assistance program.

How is Auvelity different from regular bupropion?

It combines bupropion with dextromethorphan for a different, faster-acting mechanism. The bupropion component also raises dextromethorphan levels. It is approved as a specific product; generic bupropion alone is not equivalent.

Can I use the Auvelity card on Medicare?

No — commercial insurance only. Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.
