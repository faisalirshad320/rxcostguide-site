Vraylar cost, coupon & copay card

Vraylar (cariprazine) is a once-daily atypical antipsychotic used for bipolar I disorder, schizophrenia and as an add-on for major depression — and one of the drugs selected for a Medicare-negotiated price in 2027.

The short answer

Vraylar costs about $1,550 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $0 a month with the Vraylar Savings Card. There is no generic yet. Vraylar is in the second Medicare negotiation cycle, with a negotiated price effective 1 January 2027 — until then Medicare patients pay their plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.

The Vraylar copay card

The Vraylar Savings Card can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $0 per month.

The card is for patients with commercial insurance — it cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid or any government plan. An annual maximum applies. Enroll on the official Vraylar savings page or ask your prescriber's office, which often has the card on hand.

Vraylar cost with Medicare

Vraylar was selected in the second round of Medicare drug-price negotiation, with a Maximum Fair Price taking effect 1 January 2027. For 2026, Medicare patients pay their Part D plan's cost-share, capped by the $2,100 annual out-of-pocket maximum. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare, so the Part D cap is the key protection until the negotiated price arrives.

Patient assistance for Vraylar

AbbVie provides Vraylar free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through myAbbVie Assist. The application requires proof of income and a prescription.

Cash-pay strategies for Vraylar

There is no generic cariprazine, so cash payers should compare GoodRx and SingleCare against the pharmacy price, and ask whether generic lurasidone (Latuda) or another generic antipsychotic is a clinically reasonable, far cheaper option. Income-eligible uninsured patients can get Vraylar free through myAbbVie Assist.

Cheaper alternatives to Vraylar

The biggest cost lever is a generic: generic lurasidone is a far cheaper second-generation antipsychotic for many indications. Other brand options (Rexulti, Caplyta, Abilify — which has a generic, aripiprazole) differ clinically. Any switch is a decision for your prescriber, not a price comparison.

Psychiatric drugs are where I most often see patients quietly skipping doses because of cost — which is the worst possible outcome, because these are drugs where consistency is everything. The honest conversation to have with your prescriber is: is a generic like lurasidone or aripiprazole reasonable for me? If the brand is genuinely needed, the savings card (commercial) or myAbbVie Assist (uninsured) usually makes it affordable — so cost should rarely be the reason a dose is missed.

Vraylar cost — frequently asked questions

Is there a generic for Vraylar?

Not yet. Cariprazine is still brand-only in the US. Generic lurasidone (Latuda) and generic aripiprazole (Abilify) are cheaper second-generation antipsychotics, though they are different drugs — any switch is a clinical decision.

How much is Vraylar with the savings card?

As little as $0 a month if you have commercial insurance. The card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

When does Vraylar get a Medicare-negotiated price?

Vraylar is in the second negotiation cycle, with a Medicare price effective 1 January 2027. For 2026, Medicare patients pay plan cost-share under the $2,100 Part D cap.

What does Vraylar cost without insurance?

About $1,550 a month at list price. Uninsured, income-eligible patients can often get it free through myAbbVie Assist; otherwise compare discount cards or ask about a generic alternative.

Can I use the Vraylar savings card on Medicare?

No — it is for commercial insurance only. Medicare patients rely on their plan, the $2,100 Part D cap, and from 2027 the negotiated price.
