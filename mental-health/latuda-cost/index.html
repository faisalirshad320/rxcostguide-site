Latuda cost & generic lurasidone

Latuda (lurasidone) is an atypical antipsychotic for schizophrenia and bipolar depression — and, unlike most of its class, it is now available as a much cheaper generic.

The short answer

Branded Latuda lists around $1,600 a month, but generic lurasidone is far cheaper — often $30–$60 a month with a discount card — and is the single biggest reason this hub exists. Generic lurasidone is usually the cheapest second-generation antipsychotic, so it is worth asking your prescriber and pharmacist about it first.

The Latuda copay card

The Latuda savings card can lower cost for commercially insured patients, but generic lurasidone is often cheaper still.

The brand savings card is for commercial insurance only and cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. Before using it, price generic lurasidone with GoodRx or SingleCare — for many patients the generic beats the branded card.

Latuda cost with Medicare

Latuda has no Medicare-negotiated price, but it does not need one: generic lurasidone sits on the lowest-cost tiers of most Part D plans, so Medicare patients usually pay very little. The $2,100 annual out-of-pocket cap applies on top.

Patient assistance for Latuda

The manufacturer offers assistance for uninsured, income-eligible patients, but for most people generic lurasidone with a discount card is already inexpensive enough that assistance is unnecessary.

Cash-pay strategies for Latuda

This is the easy one: ask the pharmacist for generic lurasidone and price it with GoodRx or SingleCare — it is often $30–$60 a month, far below brand Latuda. Take it with food (at least 350 calories), as absorption drops sharply on an empty stomach, which also protects the value of every dose you pay for.

Cheaper alternatives to Latuda

Generic lurasidone is itself the cheap alternative to brand-only antipsychotics like Vraylar, Rexulti and Caplyta. Generic aripiprazole and quetiapine are other low-cost options. Which is right for you is a clinical decision.

Latuda going generic was genuinely good news for patients, and yet I still see people filling the brand at full price out of habit. The one practical nuance: lurasidone must be taken with a real meal (around 350 calories) or the body absorbs far less of it — so the cheap generic only works properly if it is taken correctly. That single instruction protects both the clinical effect and the money.

Latuda cost — frequently asked questions

Is generic Latuda available?

Yes. Generic lurasidone is widely available and much cheaper than brand Latuda — often $30–$60 a month with a discount card. Ask your pharmacist for it.

How much is generic lurasidone?

Commonly around $30–$60 a month with a GoodRx or SingleCare coupon, though it varies by pharmacy and dose. That is a fraction of the ~$1,600 brand list price.

Why must Latuda be taken with food?

Lurasidone is absorbed far better with a meal of at least 350 calories. Taken on an empty stomach, blood levels can be roughly half — so take it with food to get the full effect of every dose.

Is Latuda cheaper than Vraylar or Caplyta?

Yes, by a wide margin, because Latuda has a generic (lurasidone) and Vraylar and Caplyta do not. For a cost-pressed patient, generic lurasidone is usually worth discussing first.

Does Medicare cover generic lurasidone?

Yes — it sits on low-cost tiers of most Part D plans, so Medicare patients typically pay little, under the $2,100 annual cap.
