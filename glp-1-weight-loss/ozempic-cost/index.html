Ozempic cost with Medicare

Ozempic (semaglutide) is in the second Medicare negotiation cycle, with a negotiated price effective 1 January 2027. In 2026, Medicare Part D covers it for type 2 diabetes (not weight loss) at your plan's cost-share, capped by the $2,100 Part D out-of-pocket maximum. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

Patient assistance for Ozempic

Novo Nordisk provides Ozempic free to uninsured, income-eligible patients through its patient-assistance program, accessed via NovoCare.

NovoCare / Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance

Cash-pay strategies for Ozempic

Without insurance, compare discount-card prices and ask NovoCare about patient assistance if your income qualifies. Note that Ozempic is a diabetes drug — if you're seeking semaglutide for weight loss, Wegovy (and its self-pay route) is the on-label option to discuss with your doctor.

Cheaper alternatives to Ozempic

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is the main once-weekly diabetes alternative, also with a 2027 negotiated price. See our [Mounjaro vs Ozempic comparison](/glp-1-weight-loss/mounjaro-vs-ozempic/). For weight loss specifically, Wegovy is the on-label semaglutide.

Ozempic being prescribed off-label for weight loss is the source of a lot of coverage denials. Plans and Medicare cover it for diabetes; when the prescription is really for weight loss, the on-label choice is Wegovy. Getting the indication right on the prescription is often what unlocks coverage — or clarifies why it was denied.

Ozempic cost — frequently asked questions

How much is Ozempic without insurance?

About $970 a month at list price. Discount cards may lower it somewhat; income-eligible uninsured patients can apply for free medication through NovoCare.

Does Medicare cover Ozempic?

Yes, for type 2 diabetes, subject to your Part D plan. A Medicare-negotiated price takes effect in 2027. Medicare does not cover it for weight loss.

How do I get Ozempic for $25?

If you have commercial insurance that covers Ozempic, the manufacturer savings card can bring your cost to about $25 a month. It cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is Ozempic the same as Wegovy?

Both are semaglutide. Ozempic is approved for type 2 diabetes; Wegovy for weight loss. Coverage and pricing follow the indication.

When will Ozempic have a Medicare-negotiated price?

Semaglutide is in the second negotiation cycle, with a Medicare price effective 1 January 2027.
