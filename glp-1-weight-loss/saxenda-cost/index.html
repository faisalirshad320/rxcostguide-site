Saxenda cost, coupon & generic options

Saxenda (liraglutide) is a daily GLP-1 injection for chronic weight management — an older option now facing generic competition.

Manufacturer copay cardPatient assistance
Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Branded Saxenda costs about $1,350 a month at list price. Commercially insured patients can use the Saxenda savings card, but the bigger story is generic liraglutide, which is emerging and much cheaper. Medicare does not cover it for weight loss.

Saxenda cost with Medicare

Medicare does not cover Saxenda for weight loss. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare. Where a generic liraglutide is stocked, a plan may price it lower for covered indications.

Cash-pay strategies for Saxenda

The main lever is the emerging generic liraglutide — ask your pharmacist and price it with a discount card. Saxenda is dosed daily, so it uses more drug than the weekly options; factor that into the monthly cost when comparing.

Cheaper alternatives to Saxenda

Wegovy and Zepbound are the more potent weekly options for weight loss, with their own self-pay routes. Saxenda's advantage is the arrival of a cheaper generic; its disadvantage is daily dosing and generally more modest average weight loss.

Saxenda shows how a category evolves: a once-daily first-generation drug being priced out by more effective weekly agents on one side and its own generic on the other. For a cost-focused patient, generic liraglutide may be the value option — but discuss whether a weekly drug would suit you better clinically.

Saxenda cost — frequently asked questions

Is there a generic for Saxenda?

Generic liraglutide is emerging in the US. Where available it is much cheaper than branded Saxenda — ask your pharmacist.

How much is Saxenda without insurance?

Branded Saxenda is about $1,350 a month at list price. Generic liraglutide, where available, is considerably less.

Does Medicare cover Saxenda?

No, not for weight loss. Copay cards also cannot be used with Medicare.

Is Saxenda as effective as Wegovy?

Trials generally show greater average weight loss with weekly semaglutide (Wegovy) than daily liraglutide (Saxenda), but individual response varies. The choice is your doctor's.

Why is Saxenda daily when others are weekly?

Liraglutide is a shorter-acting, older GLP-1 that requires daily dosing. Newer molecules (semaglutide, tirzepatide) last a week per dose.
