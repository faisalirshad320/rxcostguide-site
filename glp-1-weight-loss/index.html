What GLP-1 and weight-loss medications cost, and how to pay less

GLP-1 drugs are the most-searched — and most expensive — prescriptions in America right now. List prices near $1,000–$1,350 a month have been partly offset by new lower-cost self-pay options and savings cards.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Branded GLP-1 weight-loss drugs run roughly $1,000 to $1,350 a month at list price. The cheapest routes have shifted: manufacturers now sell lower-cost self-pay vials (Zepbound and Wegovy) that undercut the pen list price, and savings cards can bring commercially insured patients to as little as $0–$25. Insurance coverage for weight loss is inconsistent, and Medicare does not cover these drugs for weight loss (only for diabetes or, in some cases, heart-disease risk).

Self-pay vials changed the cash price

The biggest 2026 shift is manufacturer self-pay programs. Lilly sells Zepbound single-dose vials through LillyDirect, and Novo Nordisk sells Wegovy through NovoCare/self-pay, both well below the roughly $1,000–$1,350 pen list price. For cash payers, these are usually the cheapest legitimate route — and far safer than unregulated "compounded" sellers.

Coverage and Medicare

Commercial coverage for weight loss is patchy — many plans exclude it or require documentation of BMI and prior attempts. Medicare does not cover GLP-1 drugs for weight loss, though it covers the same molecules for diabetes (Ozempic, Mounjaro) and, in limited cases, for cardiovascular risk. Because copay cards need commercial insurance, uninsured and Medicare patients rely on self-pay vials or assistance.
