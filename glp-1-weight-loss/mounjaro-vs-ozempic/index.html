Mounjaro vs Ozempic: cost, coverage & which is right for you

The two leading once-weekly injectables for type 2 diabetes, compared on what drives your out-of-pocket cost in 2026.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) lists near $1,080/month, Ozempic (semaglutide) near $970/month. For commercially insured patients, both savings cards can reach about $25/month where the plan covers them. Both are in the 2027 Medicare negotiation cycle for diabetes. Mounjaro also has self-pay vials (as Zepbound) that Ozempic does not directly match.

When Mounjaro tends to be the choice

Mounjaro (tirzepatide) acts on two hormone pathways (GIP and GLP-1) and in head-to-head diabetes trials produced strong A1c and weight results. Its molecule also has the widely used self-pay vial route via Zepbound/LillyDirect.

When Ozempic tends to be the choice

Ozempic (semaglutide) has the longer track record and very broad formulary coverage for diabetes, which can make it easier to get approved. Its list price is slightly lower.

Which is cheaper for you?

Commercial insurance: whichever your plan covers on the better tier — both cards reach ~$25. Cash/uninsured: the tirzepatide self-pay vials (LillyDirect) are often the cheapest reliable route. Medicare: both are plan cost-share until their 2027 negotiated prices, capped by the $2,100 Part D maximum. Never switch for a small price gap without your doctor.

FAQ

Is Mounjaro or Ozempic cheaper?

List prices are close (Mounjaro ~$1,080, Ozempic ~$970). With commercial coverage both cards reach ~$25/month. For cash payers, tirzepatide self-pay vials (LillyDirect) are often the cheapest route.

Which is more effective for diabetes?

Head-to-head trials generally favor tirzepatide (Mounjaro) on A1c and weight, but individual response varies and the choice is your doctor's. This page compares cost.

Do both have Medicare-negotiated prices?

Yes — both semaglutide and tirzepatide are in the second cycle, with negotiated prices effective 1 January 2027 for their diabetes indications.
