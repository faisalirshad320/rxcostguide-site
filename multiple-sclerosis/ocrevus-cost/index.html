Ocrevus cost, coupon & copay card

Ocrevus (ocrelizumab) is a CD20-targeting monoclonal antibody given by IV infusion every 6 months to treat relapsing MS and primary progressive MS in adults.

The short answer

Ocrevus has a list price of ~$21,000 per infusion (about $42,000/yr), before infusion-center charges. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per infusion with the Ocrevus Co-Pay Program, which covers up to $20,000/yr for drug plus infusion support. On Medicare, the copay card cannot be used, and because Ocrevus is a clinic-administered infusion it is usually billed under Part B rather than Part D, so the $2,100 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap often does not apply; the Genentech Patient Foundation may help eligible patients who are uninsured or underinsured.

Ocrevus is given as an IV infusion in a clinic or infusion center, with a split starting dose and then one infusion every 6 months. Your total cost depends on the drug's high list price, plus facility and administration fees, and on whether your plan bills it under a medical benefit or a pharmacy benefit. Prior authorization is common, so coverage details and out-of-pocket costs vary widely by plan.

The Ocrevus copay card

Most commercially insured patients can lower their Ocrevus costs with the Ocrevus Co-Pay Program.

The Ocrevus Co-Pay Program can bring your out-of-pocket drug cost down to as little as $0 per infusion. The program also includes support for infusion costs, up to an annual maximum. It is for patients with commercial insurance only. People with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government coverage cannot use it. Enroll through the official Ocrevus financial support page before your next infusion. Your infusion site may be able to help with the paperwork.

Ocrevus cost with Medicare

Ocrevus is not among the drugs with a Medicare negotiated price, so there is no maximum fair price. Because it is given as an infusion in a clinic, Medicare usually covers it under Part B, where coinsurance applies after the deductible. The $2,100 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap applies to Part D drugs, so it often does not apply to Ocrevus infusions. A Medigap policy or a Medicare Advantage plan's out-of-pocket limit can reduce your costs. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries. Medicare patients with limited means can look into the Genentech Patient Foundation.

Patient assistance for Ocrevus

The Genentech Patient Foundation may provide Ocrevus at no cost to eligible patients. This includes people without insurance and some people whose insurance does not cover the drug or leaves costs they cannot afford. Eligibility depends on income and insurance status. Your neurologist's office usually submits the application with you.

Cash-pay strategies for Ocrevus

Without insurance, Ocrevus costs about ~$21,000 per infusion (about $42,000/yr) at list price, plus infusion-center fees. Retail pharmacy discount cards generally do not apply, because Ocrevus is a clinic-administered infusion. The Genentech Patient Foundation is the main route for uninsured patients, and there is currently no generic or biosimilar version.

Cheaper alternatives to Ocrevus

Other disease-modifying therapies for MS include other anti-CD20 antibodies, oral agents, and older injectable options, and some of these may cost less depending on your coverage. Your neurologist can help weigh effectiveness, safety, and cost for your type of MS.

I tell patients to start the coverage process early, because prior authorization and infusion-site scheduling for Ocrevus can take weeks. If you have commercial insurance, enroll in the co-pay program before your first infusion, and ask the infusion center how it bills facility fees, since those charges can add up separately from the drug. If you're on Medicare, check whether your coverage includes supplemental protection for Part B coinsurance.

Ocrevus cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Ocrevus cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per infusion with the Ocrevus Co-Pay Program. The program covers up to $20,000/yr for drug plus infusion support.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Ocrevus?

No. There is currently no generic or biosimilar version of Ocrevus (ocrelizumab) available in the US.

How much does Ocrevus cost without insurance?

The list price is ~$21,000 per infusion (about $42,000/yr). Infusion-center fees are extra. Uninsured patients can apply to the Genentech Patient Foundation, which may provide the drug at no cost if they qualify.

Does Medicare cover Ocrevus, and does the $2,100 cap apply?

Medicare usually covers Ocrevus under Part B because it is given as a clinic infusion. The $2,100 2026 cap applies only to Part D drugs, so it often does not apply to Ocrevus. Costs vary by plan and supplemental coverage. Ocrevus does not have a Medicare negotiated price, and copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Ocrevus Co-Pay Program?

Enroll through the official Ocrevus financial support page at https://www.ocrevus.com/patient/financial-support/assistance-options.html. You must have commercial insurance. People with Medicare or Medicaid are not eligible.
