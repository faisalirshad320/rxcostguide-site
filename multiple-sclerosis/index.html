What MS medications cost, and how to pay less

Disease-modifying therapies for multiple sclerosis and related neuroimmune conditions are among the most expensive drugs in medicine — but for commercially insured patients the manufacturer copay program usually does the heavy lifting.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-13
The short answer

MS disease-modifying therapies run from roughly $9,000 a month (oral and self-injected) to tens of thousands per infusion. Commercially insured patients can often pay $0 with the manufacturer copay program. Medicare patients rely on Part D and the $2,100 out-of-pocket cap; uninsured patients should look at each maker’s patient-assistance foundation.

Why these drugs cost so much

MS disease-modifying therapies are biologics or specialized orals with no low-cost generics, so list prices are high. What you actually pay depends almost entirely on your insurance type, not the sticker price.

Copay cards do the heavy lifting

Every branded drug here has a manufacturer copay program that can bring commercially insured patients to $0 or close to it. As always, these programs are barred for Medicare and Medicaid patients — the most common reason a card is declined.
