Tysabri cost, coupon & copay card

Tysabri (natalizumab) is an IV infusion that treats relapsing multiple sclerosis and moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease in adults.

The short answer

Tysabri's list price is ~$8,681 per infusion, but commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 with the Tysabri Biogen Copay Program. Because Tysabri is infused in a clinic, Medicare usually covers it under Part B rather than Part D, so the $2,100 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap generally does not apply. Patients without coverage can contact Biogen Support Services for help, and a natalizumab biosimilar (Tyruko) is also available.

Tysabri is given as an IV infusion at an infusion center, and it can only be prescribed through a restricted safety (REMS) program because of the risk of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML). The total cost depends on the drug's high list price, how often you receive infusions, and whether your plan bills it as a medical (infusion) benefit or a pharmacy benefit. Separate infusion-center facility and administration fees can add to what you pay, and these vary by site and plan.

The Tysabri copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 with the Tysabri Biogen Copay Program.

The Biogen copay program helps cover out-of-pocket costs for Tysabri for eligible patients with commercial (employer or marketplace) insurance. A program maximum applies per prescription per year, so check the program terms for current limits. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance.

Tysabri cost with Medicare

Tysabri was not selected for Medicare drug price negotiation, so there is no negotiated maximum fair price. Because it is infused in a clinic, Medicare usually covers Tysabri under Part B, where your cost depends on Part B coinsurance and any Medigap or Medicare Advantage coverage. The $2,100 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap applies only to drugs covered under Part D. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, but Biogen Support Services or independent charitable foundations may be able to help.

Patient assistance for Tysabri

Biogen Support Services helps patients with insurance benefit checks, prior authorization support, and financial assistance options for Tysabri. Uninsured or underinsured patients, including some who cannot use the copay card, should contact the program to see what help they may qualify for.

Cash-pay strategies for Tysabri

Without insurance, Tysabri has a high list price of ~$8,681 per infusion, plus separate infusion-center fees. Retail pharmacy discount cards usually offer little help for clinic-administered infusions like this one, so contacting Biogen Support Services about patient assistance is typically the most useful first step. Ask your neurologist or gastroenterologist whether the natalizumab biosimilar Tyruko is covered or priced lower in your situation.

Cheaper alternatives to Tysabri

A natalizumab biosimilar (Tyruko) is available, and other disease-modifying therapies for relapsing MS and advanced therapies for Crohn's disease may be options depending on your history and JCV antibody status. Coverage and out-of-pocket costs vary by plan, so compare options with your specialist and insurer.

I tell patients that the sticker price of Tysabri rarely reflects what they will actually pay, because most commercial plans cover it and the Biogen copay program can bring costs down substantially. Before the first infusion, I recommend calling both your insurer and the infusion center to ask whether it is billed under the medical or pharmacy benefit and what facility fees apply. I also remind patients on Medicare that copay cards are off-limits, so they should ask about Biogen Support Services and charitable foundations early, before bills arrive.

Tysabri cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Tysabri cost with the copay card?

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 with the Tysabri Biogen Copay Program. A program maximum applies per prescription per year, and the card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Tysabri?

There is no generic, but a natalizumab biosimilar (Tyruko) is available. Ask your doctor and insurer whether it is covered and whether it would cost you less.

How much does Tysabri cost without insurance?

The list price is ~$8,681 per infusion, and infusion-center facility and administration fees are extra. Uninsured patients should contact Biogen Support Services about financial assistance.

Does Medicare cover Tysabri?

Yes, Medicare usually covers Tysabri under Part B because it is infused in a clinic, and your cost depends on your Part B coinsurance and supplemental coverage. The $2,100 2026 Part D cap applies only to Part D drugs, Tysabri was not selected for Medicare price negotiation, and manufacturer copay cards cannot be used with Medicare.

How do I enroll in the Tysabri copay program?

You can enroll online at https://biogencopayprogram.com/Home/Enrollment?br=tysabri if you have commercial insurance. For other help, including benefit checks and assistance options, contact Biogen Support Services at https://www.biogenoptions.com/en_us/home/biogen-support-services/overview.html.
