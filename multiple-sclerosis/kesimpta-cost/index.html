Kesimpta cost, coupon & copay card

Kesimpta (ofatumumab) is a CD20 monoclonal antibody, given as a monthly self-injection at home, used to treat adults with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis.

The short answer

Kesimpta has a list price of about ~$9,900/mo, but most people with commercial insurance pay as little as $0 per month with the Kesimpta Access Card, which covers up to $18,000/yr. On Medicare Part D, the copay card can't be used, but your yearly out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. If you're uninsured or underinsured, the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation may provide the drug for free if you qualify.

Kesimpta is a disease-modifying therapy for relapsing MS that patients inject themselves at home once a month after an initial loading period, avoiding the infusion-center visits required by some other CD20 therapies. Because it is a brand-name biologic with no biosimilar available, its list price is high, and what you actually pay depends mostly on your insurance type, your plan's specialty-tier coverage, and whether you qualify for manufacturer support.

The Kesimpta copay card

With commercial insurance, the Kesimpta Access Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per month.

The Kesimpta Access Card from Novartis helps pay the out-of-pocket cost of Kesimpta for people with commercial (private or employer) insurance. The program pays up to $18,000/yr toward your costs. It is not available to anyone with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. You can enroll through the official Kesimpta savings and support page, and the program's patient support team can also help with insurance approvals.

Kesimpta cost with Medicare

Kesimpta is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price, so your cost depends on your Part D plan's coverage and tier. In 2026, Medicare Part D caps total out-of-pocket spending on covered drugs at $2,100 for the year, which limits what you pay for a high-cost drug like Kesimpta. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare patients, so the Kesimpta Access Card can't be used. Some patients may still qualify for the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation or Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy).

Patient assistance for Kesimpta

The Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation may provide Kesimpta free to eligible patients who are uninsured or whose coverage doesn't adequately cover the drug. Eligibility depends on factors such as household income and insurance status, and your prescriber usually helps complete the application.

Cash-pay strategies for Kesimpta

Without insurance, Kesimpta's list price is about ~$9,900/mo, and pharmacy discount cards typically offer limited savings on specialty biologics like this one. The best first step is usually to apply to the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation. Your neurologist can also discuss lower-cost MS therapies, including some that are available as generics.

Cheaper alternatives to Kesimpta

Other disease-modifying options for relapsing MS include infused CD20 antibodies and oral or injectable therapies, several of which are available as lower-cost generics. Which alternative fits best depends on disease activity, safety considerations, and your coverage, so review options with your neurologist.

I tell patients not to let Kesimpta's list price scare them off before checking what they will actually pay, because insurance plus the Access Card or the Novartis foundation often changes the picture completely. I also suggest calling the manufacturer's patient support line early, since prior authorization and specialty-pharmacy setup can delay a first shipment. If you have Medicare, plan around the 2026 Part D cap, and ask your plan whether you can spread your out-of-pocket costs across the year.

Kesimpta cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Kesimpta cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, the Kesimpta Access Card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per month, up to a program maximum of $18,000/yr. It is not available for Medicare or Medicaid patients.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Kesimpta?

No. There is currently no generic or biosimilar version of Kesimpta (ofatumumab); it is available only as the brand-name product from Novartis.

How much is Kesimpta without insurance?

Kesimpta's list price is about ~$9,900/mo. Uninsured patients should apply to the Novartis Patient Assistance Foundation, which may provide the drug free to those who qualify.

Does Medicare cover Kesimpta, and what will I pay?

Kesimpta may be covered under Medicare Part D, and your cost varies by plan. In 2026, Part D caps your total yearly out-of-pocket drug spending at $2,100. The Kesimpta Access Card can't be used with Medicare, and Kesimpta does not have a Medicare-negotiated price.

How do I enroll in the Kesimpta Access Card?

You can enroll through the official Kesimpta savings and support page at https://www.kesimpta.com/savings-and-support/patient-support. You'll need commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients aren't eligible.
