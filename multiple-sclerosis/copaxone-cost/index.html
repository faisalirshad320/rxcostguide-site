Copaxone cost, coupon & copay card

Copaxone (glatiramer acetate) is a self-injected immunomodulator from Teva used to treat adults with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis.

The short answer

Copaxone has a list price of about ~$6,500–7,000/mo without insurance. With commercial insurance, the Copaxone Co-Pay Solutions card can bring your cost down to as little as $0 per prescription (annual limits apply). On Medicare Part D, the card can't be used, but your out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. A generic glatiramer acetate is also available.

Copaxone is a disease-modifying therapy for relapsing MS that patients inject under the skin at home on a regular, ongoing schedule. Because it is taken long-term, the high monthly list price adds up quickly, and what you actually pay depends heavily on your insurance type, your plan's formulary, and whether it prefers the brand or the generic.

The Copaxone copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per prescription with Copaxone Co-Pay Solutions.

The Copaxone Co-Pay Solutions program helps cover out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients whose commercial or private insurance covers Copaxone. Annual limits apply, so check the program terms for your plan year. The card cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance.

Copaxone cost with Medicare

Copaxone has not been selected for Medicare drug price negotiation, so there is no Maximum Fair Price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan, but in 2026 your total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 for the year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so ask about Teva's Shared Solutions program or whether your plan prefers the generic.

Patient assistance for Copaxone

Shared Solutions is Teva's patient support program for Copaxone. It can help with insurance questions, copay assistance enrollment, and affordability options for eligible patients, including those who are uninsured or underinsured. Contact the program directly to confirm eligibility.

Cash-pay strategies for Copaxone

Without insurance, Copaxone's list price is high at roughly ~$6,500–7,000/mo. Ask your doctor whether the generic glatiramer acetate is appropriate, compare pharmacy discount card prices, and contact Teva's Shared Solutions program to see if you qualify for assistance.

Cheaper alternatives to Copaxone

A generic glatiramer acetate is available and is often preferred by insurers. Other disease-modifying therapies for relapsing MS exist, including oral and infusion options, and your neurologist can help weigh cost, coverage, and fit.

I tell patients to check early whether their plan covers brand Copaxone or prefers generic glatiramer acetate, because that single formulary detail often matters most for their monthly cost. If you have commercial insurance, enroll in the copay program before your first fill. If you're on Medicare, remember the 2026 out-of-pocket cap and call Shared Solutions rather than trying to use a coupon.

Copaxone cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Copaxone cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, the Copaxone Co-Pay Solutions card can lower your cost to as little as $0 per prescription. Annual limits apply.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Copaxone?

Yes, a generic glatiramer acetate is available. There is no biosimilar, because glatiramer acetate is not regulated as a biologic.

How much is Copaxone without insurance?

The approximate list price is ~$6,500–7,000/mo. Uninsured patients can ask about the generic, compare discount card prices, and contact Teva's Shared Solutions program for help.

Does Medicare cover Copaxone, and can I use the copay card?

Many Medicare Part D plans cover glatiramer acetate, though costs vary by plan. Your out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026. The copay card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. Copaxone has not been selected for Medicare price negotiation.

How do I enroll in the Copaxone copay program?

Enroll through the official Copaxone copay assistance page at https://www.copaxone.com/shared-solutions/copay-assistance. You must have commercial insurance. For broader support, visit Shared Solutions at https://www.copaxone.com/shared-solutions.
