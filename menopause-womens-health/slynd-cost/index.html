Slynd cost, coupon & copay card

Slynd (drospirenone) is a brand-name, estrogen-free, progestin-only birth control pill used to prevent pregnancy.

Manufacturer copay card
Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-13
The short answer

Slynd has a list price of about ~$197/mo without insurance. If you have commercial insurance, the Slynd Savings Program can bring your cost to $25 per 1- or 3-month supply. This is not $0, and the card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. On Medicare Part D, your cost depends on your plan's formulary, and 2026 out-of-pocket drug spending is capped at $2,100.

Slynd is taken once daily as an estrogen-free contraceptive option. It is sometimes chosen for people who should avoid estrogen. Because it is a brand-name product, your out-of-pocket cost depends heavily on whether your insurance plan covers it, which tier it sits on, and whether you can use the manufacturer's savings program.

The Slynd copay card

With commercial insurance, the Slynd Savings Program can lower your cost to $25 per 1- or 3-month supply.

The Slynd Savings Program from Exeltis is for patients with commercial (private or employer) insurance. With the program, you pay $25 per 1- or 3-month supply. That is not $0. The program cannot be used by people with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Program terms apply, so check the official enrollment page for current details before you fill your prescription.

Slynd cost with Medicare

Slynd is not on the list of drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Whether a Part D plan covers it, and what you pay, varies by plan formulary and tier. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards from being used with Medicare. In 2026, the Part D out-of-pocket cap of $2,100 limits your total yearly spending on covered drugs.

Cash-pay strategies for Slynd

Without insurance, Slynd's list price is about ~$197/mo, and actual pharmacy prices vary. Pharmacy discount cards may lower the cash price, so compare prices between pharmacies. No manufacturer patient-assistance program is listed. Generic progestin-only and combined pills are often much cheaper, so ask your prescriber whether one of them would suit you.

Cheaper alternatives to Slynd

Other birth control options include generic progestin-only pills, combined estrogen-progestin pills, and long-acting methods such as IUDs or implants. Many of these may cost less, depending on your coverage and medical history.

I tell patients that Slynd is a useful estrogen-free option, but it is a brand-name pill. Cost is often the deciding factor. If you have commercial insurance, enroll in the savings program before your first fill and confirm your plan covers it. If you are paying cash, we can usually find a lower-cost progestin-only or other contraceptive that fits your health needs.

Slynd cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Slynd cost with the savings card?

Eligible patients with commercial insurance can pay $25 per 1- or 3-month supply with the Slynd Savings Program. This is not $0, and program terms apply.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Slynd?

Slynd is not a biologic, so a biosimilar does not apply. Slynd is sold as a brand-name product. Ask your pharmacist whether a lower-cost generic version is available to you. Your prescriber can also suggest other generic progestin-only pills.

How much is Slynd without insurance?

The approximate list price is ~$197/mo. Pharmacy discount cards and comparing pharmacies may lower your cash price. No manufacturer patient-assistance program is listed.

Does Medicare cover Slynd, and can I use the copay card?

Coverage under Medicare Part D varies by plan. Copay cards cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket costs for covered drugs are capped at $2,100 per year.

How do I enroll in the Slynd Savings Program?

Enroll through the official Slynd savings page at https://hcp.slynd.com/slynd-patient-savings-program/. You must have commercial insurance. People with Medicare or Medicaid are not eligible.
