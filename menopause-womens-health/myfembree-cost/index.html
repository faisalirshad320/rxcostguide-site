Myfembree cost, coupon & copay card

Myfembree (relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone) is a once-daily oral tablet for premenopausal women with heavy menstrual bleeding from uterine fibroids or moderate to severe endometriosis pain.

The short answer

Myfembree has a list price of about ~$1,302/mo, and there is no generic or biosimilar. If you have commercial insurance, the Myfembree Copay Assistance Program can lower your cost to $5 per prescription (not $0), with a benefit of up to $5,000/yr. On Medicare Part D, the copay card can't be used, but your total out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026.

Myfembree combines relugolix, which lowers the body's own estrogen production, with low-dose estradiol and norethindrone acetate to help limit side effects such as bone loss and hot flashes. It is a brand-only medication, so the high list price is the main cost driver. What you actually pay depends on your insurance, your plan's formulary tier and prior-authorization rules, and whether you qualify for the manufacturer copay program.

The Myfembree copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay $5 per prescription with the Myfembree Copay Assistance Program.

The Myfembree Copay Assistance Program lowers out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients with commercial (private or employer) insurance. Your cost is $5 per prescription, not $0. The program covers up to $5,000/yr, and you may pay more once that limit is reached. It cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Myfembree cost with Medicare

Myfembree is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Coverage and cost-sharing under Medicare Part D vary by plan and formulary tier. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the $5 copay program cannot be used. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 for the year, which limits total annual spending for people who need a high-cost brand medication.

Cash-pay strategies for Myfembree

Without insurance, Myfembree's list price is about ~$1,302/mo, and it has no generic. Pharmacy discount cards may reduce the cash price somewhat, but savings vary by pharmacy, so compare prices before filling. No manufacturer free-drug program is listed, so if the cost is out of reach, ask your clinician about lower-cost treatment options.

Cheaper alternatives to Myfembree

Other options for fibroid-related heavy bleeding or endometriosis pain include other GnRH antagonist products, hormonal contraceptives, progestin therapies such as a levonorgestrel IUD, NSAIDs, and procedural or surgical treatments. Many of these are available as lower-cost generics, and the best choice depends on your diagnosis, goals, and medical history.

I tell patients that Myfembree is a brand-only medication, so the first step is checking whether their plan covers it and whether prior authorization is needed. Commercially insured patients should enroll in the copay program before the first fill, because the cost is $5 per prescription, not $0. I also remind patients that treatment is limited to 24 months because of bone-loss risk, so it is worth planning longer-term options early.

Myfembree cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Myfembree cost with the copay card?

With the Myfembree Copay Assistance Program, eligible commercially insured patients pay $5 per prescription (not $0). The program covers up to $5,000/yr.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Myfembree?

No. Myfembree is available only as a brand-name product, and no generic or biosimilar version is available.

How much does Myfembree cost without insurance?

The list price is about ~$1,302/mo. Actual pharmacy cash prices vary, and discount cards may lower the price somewhat. No manufacturer free-drug patient-assistance program is listed.

Does Medicare cover Myfembree, and can I use the copay card?

Coverage under Medicare Part D varies by plan. The copay card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 for the year.

How do I enroll in the Myfembree copay program?

Enroll through the official Myfembree cost and support page at https://www.myfembree.com/cost-and-support/. You must have commercial insurance to qualify; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible.
