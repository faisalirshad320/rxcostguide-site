Airsupra cost, coupon & copay card

Airsupra (albuterol/budesonide) is an as-needed rescue inhaler from AstraZeneca that combines a fast-acting bronchodilator with an inhaled steroid for people with asthma.

The short answer

Airsupra has a list price of ~$500 per inhaler. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per inhaler with the Airsupra Savings Card (annual maximum applies). On Medicare Part D, the card can't be used, but your 2026 out-of-pocket spending on covered drugs is capped at $2,100, and the AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program may help if you qualify.

Airsupra is used as needed when asthma symptoms flare, so how often you refill depends on how well your asthma is controlled. Because it is a brand-name combination inhaler with no generic equivalent, the list price is high, and what you actually pay depends mostly on your insurance type, your plan's formulary tier, and whether you use the manufacturer's savings card.

The Airsupra copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per inhaler with the Airsupra Savings Card.

The Airsupra Savings Card from AstraZeneca lowers out-of-pocket costs for patients with commercial (private or employer) insurance. An annual maximum applies, so savings can run out if you refill often. The card cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. Enroll online through AstraZeneca's patient support site and show the card at the pharmacy with each fill.

Airsupra cost with Medicare

Airsupra is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Coverage and cost-sharing vary by Part D plan and formulary tier. In 2026, Part D out-of-pocket spending on covered drugs is capped at $2,100 per year, which limits your total costs if you use Airsupra regularly. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare patients, so the Airsupra Savings Card can't be used, but you may be able to apply to the AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program.

Patient assistance for Airsupra

AstraZeneca's AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program helps eligible patients who have trouble affording their AstraZeneca medicines. Eligibility depends on factors such as income and insurance status. Check the program website for current criteria and application steps.

Cash-pay strategies for Airsupra

Without insurance, Airsupra's list price is ~$500 per inhaler, and pharmacy cash prices can vary. Pharmacy discount cards may lower the price somewhat, and the AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program may help if you qualify. Ask your clinician whether a lower-cost rescue inhaler regimen is appropriate for you. Generic albuterol-only inhalers are usually much cheaper, but they lack the inhaled steroid component.

Cheaper alternatives to Airsupra

Other as-needed rescue options include generic albuterol inhalers and other bronchodilator or inhaled-steroid regimens. Whether one of these is a reasonable substitute depends on your asthma severity and your clinician's treatment plan.

I tell patients that Airsupra is meant to be used as needed, so if they're reaching for it often, that's a signal to revisit their overall asthma control rather than just refill. For commercially insured patients, I recommend enrolling in the savings card before the first fill and keeping in mind that the annual maximum can run out with frequent use. My Medicare patients should check their Part D formulary tier and know that their 2026 out-of-pocket costs are capped.

Airsupra cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Airsupra cost with the savings card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per inhaler with the Airsupra Savings Card. An annual maximum applies, and the card is not valid for Medicare or Medicaid patients.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Airsupra?

No. There is currently no generic version of Airsupra (albuterol/budesonide). Biosimilars apply only to biologic drugs, and Airsupra is not a biologic. Generic albuterol-only inhalers exist, but they do not contain budesonide.

How much is Airsupra without insurance?

Airsupra's list price is ~$500 per inhaler. Actual cash prices vary by pharmacy, and discount cards or the AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program may lower your cost if you qualify.

Does Medicare cover Airsupra, and can I use the copay card?

Coverage depends on your Part D plan's formulary. Airsupra does not have a Medicare-negotiated price. Copay cards can't be used with Medicare, but 2026 Part D out-of-pocket costs for covered drugs are capped at $2,100. You can also check eligibility for the AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program.

How do I enroll in the Airsupra Savings Card?

If you have commercial insurance, enroll online at https://www.azpatientsupport.com/home/commercially-insured.html?brand=AIRSUPRA&role=Patient, then present the card at your pharmacy when you fill your prescription.
