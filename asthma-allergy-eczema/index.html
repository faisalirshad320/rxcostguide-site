What asthma, allergy and eczema medications cost, and how to pay less

This category spans cheap inhalers with generics all the way to biologics costing thousands a month. Which savings route fits depends heavily on whether your drug is an inhaler or an injectable biologic.

Reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical Pathology PMDC 54440-SUpdated 2026-10-01
The short answer

Costs here range widely — from inhalers around $350–$650 a month (some with generics) to biologics of $1,500–$5,000+ a month. Commercially insured patients use copay cards ($0 for most); Medicare patients rely on Part D and the $2,100 cap; uninsured patients look to patient assistance. For inhalers like Symbicort, a generic is often the cheapest route.

Inhalers vs biologics

The two groups behave very differently on cost. Inhalers (Trelegy, Symbicort) are cheaper and some now have generics — often the best route. Biologics (Dupixent, Fasenra, Xolair, Nucala) cost thousands a month, so the copay card, Part D cap or patient assistance is essential.

Generics can be the cheapest route

Where a generic inhaler exists (as for Symbicort), it usually beats everything else. Always ask your pharmacist whether a generic version of your inhaler is available before assuming you need a coupon.
