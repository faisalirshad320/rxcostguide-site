Adbry cost, coupon & copay card

Adbry (tralokinumab-ldrm) is a subcutaneous IL-13 inhibitor biologic from LEO Pharma for people with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (eczema) that is not well controlled with topical therapies.

The short answer

Adbry has a list price of ~$2,000 per 2-syringe pack, so paying cash is expensive. With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per fill with the Adbry Copay Program. On Medicare Part D, the copay card cannot be used, but your total out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100 in 2026.

Adbry is a biologic injected under the skin every 2 weeks, and it is typically used long term to control eczema flares and itch. Because it is a brand-name biologic with no biosimilar available, the list price is high, and what you actually pay depends mostly on your insurance type, deductible, and whether your plan requires prior authorization.

The Adbry copay card

Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per fill with the Adbry Copay Program.

The Adbry Copay Program from LEO Pharma helps cover out-of-pocket costs for eligible patients whose commercial (employer or individual) insurance covers Adbry. The program is for commercial insurance only — it cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other government insurance. An annual program maximum applies per prescription per year, so review the program terms when you enroll.

Adbry cost with Medicare

Adbry is not among the drugs with a Medicare-negotiated price. Because it is self-injected, it is generally covered under Medicare Part D, and your cost depends on your plan's formulary tier and deductible. In 2026, total out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs is capped at $2,100 per year. Federal rules bar manufacturer copay cards for Medicare beneficiaries, so the Adbry Copay Program cannot be used; patients with limited income may want to check Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy) eligibility or the Adbry Advocate Program.

Patient assistance for Adbry

The Adbry Advocate Program is LEO Pharma's patient support program, offering help with insurance navigation, coverage questions, and access to savings options. Patients who are uninsured, underinsured, or facing coverage barriers should contact the program to see what support they qualify for.

Cash-pay strategies for Adbry

Without insurance, Adbry costs about ~$2,000 per 2-syringe pack, and generic discount cards usually do little for specialty biologics like this one. Your best options are to contact the Adbry Advocate Program about assistance, and to ask your clinician whether another treatment option might be more affordable for you.

Cheaper alternatives to Adbry

Other options for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis include other injectable biologics, oral JAK inhibitors, and optimized topical therapy; coverage and cost vary widely by plan, so it is worth checking your formulary before switching.

I tell patients to start the prior authorization and copay card enrollment at the same time as the prescription, because delays usually come from paperwork, not the pharmacy. For commercially insured patients, enrolling in the Adbry Copay Program before the first fill can make a large difference in what they pay. For my Medicare patients, I remind them that the copay card won't apply, but the 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap provides a firm ceiling on their yearly drug costs.

Adbry cost — frequently asked questions

How much does Adbry cost with the copay card?

With commercial insurance, you may pay as little as $0 per fill with the Adbry Copay Program. An annual program maximum applies per prescription per year, and the card cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Is there a generic or biosimilar for Adbry?

No. Adbry (tralokinumab-ldrm) does not currently have a biosimilar available, so it is only sold as the brand-name product.

How much does Adbry cost without insurance?

The list price is ~$2,000 per 2-syringe pack. Uninsured patients should contact the Adbry Advocate Program to ask about assistance options.

Does Medicare cover Adbry, and what will I pay?

Adbry is generally covered under Medicare Part D, with costs varying by plan. It is not a Medicare-negotiated drug, and the copay card cannot be used, but your total Part D out-of-pocket spending is capped at $2,100 in 2026.

How do I enroll in the Adbry Copay Program?

You can enroll on the official Adbry savings and support page at https://www.adbry.com/savings-and-support-programs. You must have commercial insurance; Medicare and Medicaid patients are not eligible.
